Research Lines

Unified and engineering-grounded CAD benchmark design

Defines evaluation surfaces for CAD program generation across modalities, geometric complexity, executability, design intent, manufacturability, and multi-task performance.

Open edge: Metric disagreement and ranking changes across criteria, dataset and rubric coverage for real engineering constraints, and reliability of VLM judges across tasks.
Parametric text-to-CAD program synthesis with structural and parameter grounding

Translates natural language specifications into executable CadQuery or B-rep programs while aligning topology and parameter choices to improve structural consistency.

Open edge: Generalization beyond curated datasets, robustness to ambiguous prompts, interaction cost, and evidence isolating grounding modules from base LLM code ability.
Optimization- and stepwise-based mesh-to-CAD reverse engineering

Recovers editable parametric construction sequences from meshes by iterative fitting and validation or feature-by-feature construction with intermediate geometric feedback.

Open edge: Coverage of complex topology and rich operations, sensitivity to mesh quality/noise, and isolation of optimization or feedback contribution versus learned priors.
Universal multi-modal multi-task CAD models and agents

Processes text, images, sketches, and point clouds in one framework for reconstruction, generation, and CAD QA, optionally adding clarification loops for ambiguous prompts.

Open edge: Cross-task transfer evidence, modality-shift brittleness, interaction overhead, and whether unified models match specialized models under matched training and evaluation.

Shared Direction

  • Editable parametric CAD programs, especially B-rep or CadQuery scripts, have become the dominant target representation rather than only mesh or point-cloud outputs.
  • Evaluation increasingly combines geometric fidelity, executability or validity, and design-intent or engineering criteria, but model rankings change across metrics.
  • LLM, VLM, or transformer-based program synthesis appears as a shared substrate across text-to-CAD, mesh-to-CAD, and CAD question answering.
  • Visible evidence often reports substantial improvement but lacks detailed limitations or artifact links, leaving reproducibility and failure boundaries under-specified.

Key Differences

  • Central problem formulation differs across text-to-CAD generation, mesh-to-CAD reconstruction, and multi-modal multi-task unification.
  • Supervision and inference differ among supervised fine-tuning or RL, hybrid geometric optimization, and agentic clarification plus code synthesis.
  • Evaluation targets differ among geometric fidelity or executability, manufacturability or functionality or assemblability, and broad multi-task accuracy.
  • Interaction mode differs between single-pass generation and proactive clarification, with implicit disagreement about acceptable interaction overhead.
  • Unified versus specialized models differ: specialized models can outperform under idealized inputs while unified models target broader coverage but may be brittle under modality shift.

Open Questions

  • Which representation and objective choices transfer beyond the specific benchmark families and CAD program formats used in the visible top-ranked papers?
  • How should metrics be weighted or redesigned when geometric fidelity, executability, and engineering criteria produce conflicting model rankings?
  • How much of reported improvement comes from the learned model rather than from geometric feedback, planner or checker loops, or evaluation scaffolding?
  • What are the failure boundaries under complex geometry, noisy or shifted modalities, and ambiguous prompts, and how should they be measured?
  • Does unified multimodal CAD training generalize across heterogeneous CAD tasks without losing specialized reconstruction or generation accuracy?

Research Lines

Benchmark task and dataset design

Creating shared tasks and datasets that make CAD generation and reconstruction claims comparable across multimodal inputs.

Open edge: Whether the task distribution covers real design workflows, assembly constraints, and manufacturing settings beyond aggregated existing datasets.
Metric and stress-test design

Probing geometric fidelity, program executability/validity, robustness to complexity and modality shift, and higher-level design intent.

Open edge: Alignment between proxy metrics and downstream engineering decisions, plus the reliability of automated VLM judges and human agreement.
Comparable baselines and reporting

Making results auditable by comparing specialized CAD models and general vision-language or LLM agents under shared evaluation protocols.

Open edge: Whether benchmark conclusions remain stable across implementations, compute budgets, and unverified code/dataset/artifact availability.

Shared Direction

  • Recent work converges on evaluating CAD generation with more than aggregate geometry scores, adding executability, validity, and engineering design criteria.
  • There is a shared focus on multimodal CAD program generation or reconstruction from text, images, sketches, point clouds, and meshes.
  • Staged evaluation is common: first code validity, then geometric validity, then design-intent alignment with structured specifications.
  • Benchmarks increasingly report failure cascades and sensitivity of model rankings rather than treating a single metric as sufficient.
  • Specialized CAD models may outperform general vision-language or LLM systems under idealized conditions, but robustness remains an open concern.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target differs: idealized geometric similarity, parametric program validity, or manufacturability/functionality/assemblability.
  • Supervision and validation differ: deterministic geometry/code checks, VLM judges with design-specific rubrics, human annotation, and ranking-based parameter grounding.
  • Interaction mode differs: single-shot multimodal generation versus proactive clarification before CAD code synthesis.
  • Dataset construction differs: aggregating existing mesh/CAD families versus curating real-world datasets or structured assembly-level design specifications.

Open Questions

  • How well do current geometric and validity metrics align with actual manufacturability and downstream engineering decision quality?
  • Why do model rankings change across metrics and modalities, and which evaluation settings should be trusted?
  • How reliable are VLM judges for design-intent evaluation, and how much human verification is needed across different design domains?
  • Do current benchmarks cover realistic assembly, tolerance, and manufacturing process constraints?
  • What reproducibility gaps exist in code, data, and artifact availability, and would conclusions persist under different implementations and compute budgets?

Research Lines

End-to-end multimodal CAD program generation

Converts text, image, sketch, and point-cloud inputs into executable parametric CAD programs using large multimodal models or code-generating pipelines.

Open edge: Robustness under input modality shift and geometric complexity, and engineering validity beyond program executability remain unproven.
Parametric mesh or point-to-CAD reverse engineering

Recovers editable CAD construction sequences from observed geometry through incremental operation fitting and geometric feedback.

Open edge: Invalid program ratios and coverage for complex designs, operation sets, and dataset or kernel transfer are not fully resolved.
Unified CAD benchmarks and evaluation protocols

Provides standardized evaluation across datasets, input modalities, geometric metrics, code executability, and engineering design-intent rubrics.

Open edge: Code and dataset artifact availability plus independent reproduction are verification gaps; judge reliability and metric sensitivity need further evidence.
Agentic grounding and proactive clarification

Aligns natural language specifications to geometric entities or parameters and asks targeted clarification questions before code synthesis to reduce ambiguity.

Open edge: Interaction overhead, kernel generalization, and performance outside curated text-to-CadQuery or grounding datasets are unclear.

Shared Direction

  • CAD generation is increasingly treated as executable program synthesis over procedural or hierarchical construction sequences.
  • Evaluation requires complementary signals spanning geometric fidelity, executability or validity, and engineering design intent such as manufacturability or assemblability.
  • Structure/parameter grounding or stepwise feedback improves consistency and reduces invalid outputs relative to direct sequence prediction.
  • Failures cascade across stages: executable code may still yield invalid geometry or fail engineering requirements.

Key Differences

  • Representation and task scope vary from specialized single-modality CAD generation to unified multimodal multi-task CAD systems.
  • Optimization differs: hybrid geometric feedback/IoU-driven reconstruction versus supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning versus proactive agentic clarification.
  • Grounding mechanism differs: hierarchical structure alignment and parameter perturbation, text-based B-Rep primitive grounding, or stepwise intermediate geometry comparison.
  • Evaluation target differs: geometric fidelity and program validity versus manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability judged by a VLM.
  • Interaction mode differs: proactive clarification before generation versus end-to-end automatic synthesis without disambiguation.

Open Questions

  • Can current systems transfer across CAD kernels, datasets, and infrastructure environments? Supplied evidence lacks independent reproduction artifacts and deployment details.
  • How stable are model rankings under metric choice, input modality shift, and B-rep face-count complexity? Evidence shows rankings change across metrics and specialized models are brittle under modality shift.
  • Can VLM-based design-intent judges reliably substitute for or complement human engineering review? Human validation is mentioned but not fully demonstrated.
  • What is the acceptable trade-off between proactive clarification and automation across real workflows? Evidence shows low interaction overhead in one curated setting, but generalization is unknown.
  • Where exactly do failure cascades begin for complex assemblies: code generation, geometry validity, or design-intent alignment? Current metrics capture stages separately but root causes remain open.

Research Lines

Parametric reverse engineering from non-parametric geometry, e.g., CADFit, CADENA, SOV-CAD

Recovers editable parametric CAD construction sequences from meshes, images, or orthographic projections.

Open edge: Cross-category and real-world geometry generalization, robust handling of complex operations, and transparent limitation reporting remain under-evidenced.
Unified multimodal and CAD code generation models, e.g., UniCAD, CAD-Coder, CADEvolve

Covers multiple CAD tasks such as reconstruction, generation, and question answering, or translates visual inputs into executable CAD code within one model.

Open edge: Out-of-distribution robustness, industrial-grade operation coverage, and reproducible data/model artifacts are still verification gaps.
Long-sequence generative modeling of parametric CAD, e.g., Mamba-CAD

Learns latent representations of long parametric CAD sequences and generates longer valid CAD models.

Open edge: Validity metrics alone do not establish design-intent fidelity or downstream manufacturing utility; longer-sequence evidence is still limited.
Design-intent conditioning and evaluation diagnostics, e.g., Wrong Design Intent Is Worse Than None

Tests whether completion models truly use design-intent headers and provides independent geometric assertion metrics to avoid circular evaluation.

Open edge: Current evidence is bounded to specific intents, one model checkpoint, and conditional completion regimes; broader causal evaluation is missing.

Shared Direction

  • Recent work consistently treats editable parametric CAD programs, rather than static meshes or point clouds, as the core reconstruction or generation output.
  • Geometric or syntactic validity is a shared evaluation concern, with variants such as IoU, Chamfer Distance, invalid ratio, solid similarity, and executable assertions.
  • Multiple works reuse DeepCAD, Fusion 360, and MCB as common benchmarks for cross-method comparison.
  • There is a shift toward multimodal inputs and unified task models, particularly combining image, point cloud, text, sketch, and mesh inputs.

Key Differences

  • Output representation differs: some methods emit structured parametric operation sequences, others emit executable CAD code, and others decode latent representations back to CAD.
  • Training and interaction mode differs: supervised sequence fitting, offline reinforcement learning with stepwise views, VLM-guided program evolution, self-supervised reconstruction plus GAN generation, and LoRA fine-tuning with header conditioning.
  • Evaluation emphasis differs: geometric similarity, syntax validity, invalid ratio, B-rep design-intent adherence, or data efficiency are prioritized differently.
  • Input modality and task scope differ: mesh, image, orthographic views, point cloud, sketch, text, or a unified mixture; some focus on image-to-CAD, some on point-to-CAD, and some on multi-task CAD.

Open Questions

  • How well do reported improvements transfer to real-world CAD/CAE/CAM workflows, industrial parts, and unseen operations, given only partial generalization evidence?
  • Which evaluation metric best captures editable design intent without circularity, and how should validity be balanced with geometric similarity?
  • Can long-sequence and complex-operation CAD generation be improved without losing design-intent fidelity or reproducibility?
  • Do missing code, dataset, and limitation details in several works represent verification gaps rather than absence, and how should they be resolved?

Literature review synthesis

Research Lines

Multimodal and industrial CAD program generation benchmarks

Defines controlled evaluation protocols across image, text, mesh, and 3D inputs for executable CAD programs; stratifies geometric complexity and adds engineering-oriented checks.

Open edge: Metric agreement across geometry, executability, and engineering intent; automated judge reliability; generalization to unseen part families and complex operations.
Hierarchical and grounded text-to-CAD program generation

Decomposes construction trees into topology and parameter reasoning, with explicit structure alignment, parameter grounding, or clarification to improve program validity and fidelity.

Open edge: Handling ambiguous prompts without excessive interaction; transferring grounding beyond seen template or part-family distributions; preserving complex operations like sweep and loft.
Differentiable parametric B-Rep and sequence generative models

Generates editable B-Reps or long parametric CAD sequences from weak point-level or latent supervision, reducing dependence on CAD-specific annotations.

Open edge: Exact constraint preservation, modality shift robustness, and whether point-level supervision transfers to engineering assemblies.
LLM-driven executable CAD code generation and editing

Maps natural language and images to executable CadQuery scripts; supports code QA, editing, and program synthesis verifiable by execution.

Open edge: Coarse outer geometry is often recovered but fine 3D structure and design parameters are missed; distribution shift remains after fine-tuning and reinforcement learning.

Shared Direction

  • The field treats production of executable parametric CAD programs or editable B-Reps as the central capability to verify geometric validity beyond static mesh prediction.
  • There is shared emphasis on explicit grounding: natural language or images are aligned to topology, primitive, or parameter structure before program decoding.
  • Evaluation is moving beyond single-value reconstruction error toward stratified, multi-metric protocols covering executability, compactness, and design intent.
  • Several studies identify a common failure cascade from executable code, to valid geometry, to engineering-ready design.

Key Differences

  • Representation choice varies from CadQuery scripts, construction trees, and Bézier/B-Rep patches to latent state-space sequences.
  • Supervision ranges from point-level or unannotated 3D data to curated text-CAD pairs with interactive clarification trajectories.
  • Evaluation target differs between idealized geometric fidelity and industry-oriented manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability rubrics.
  • Some methods use direct generation while others insert proactive clarification, changing the interaction and error-correction boundary.
  • Automated geometric metrics and VLM-based design-intent judges are not yet shown to agree across all tasks.

Open Questions

  • How can current text-to-CAD and image-to-CAD methods generalize to unseen industrial part families and complex operations such as sweeps and lofts?
  • Do automated metrics and VLM judges align with expert engineering evaluation across manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability?
  • Which representation and supervision combination best balances geometric fidelity, editability, and robustness under modality shift?
  • What formal or structural alignment conditions are sufficient for engineering-ready CAD rather than merely executable code or valid geometry?

Controllable CAD model generation

Enabling user control over the generation process through hierarchical neural coding, which could support design exploration.

Open edge: The evidence lacks details on the control mechanisms, evaluation metrics, and any constraints on the types of controllable edits.
Dataset creation for manufacturing processes

Providing a multimodal dataset (Slice-100K) for extrusion-based 3D printing, which can facilitate learning-based slicing and toolpath planning.

Open edge: The downstream model performance and the dataset's coverage of manufacturing variability are not discussed in the evidence.

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
