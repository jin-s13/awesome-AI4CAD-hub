Research Lines

LLM-driven parametric program generation and grounding

Generating editable CAD scripts or construction sequences from text/image inputs by grounding semantics to B-Rep primitives, parameters, and hierarchical topology.

Open edge: Robustness to ambiguous natural-language prompts, modality shift, and transfer to complex assemblies beyond curated single-part datasets.
Reverse engineering and geometry-to-CAD reconstruction

Recovering editable parametric CAD operations from meshes, point clouds, or images through iterative fitting or optimization with geometric feedback.

Open edge: Operation coverage and complexity scaling, plus whether geometric metrics capture engineering validity and design intent.
Unified multimodal and multitask CAD modeling

Handling heterogeneous CAD tasks such as reconstruction, generation, and question answering within one model and benchmark.

Open edge: Whether unification preserves task-specific robustness and how benchmark coverage affects model ranking.
Proactive and interactive CAD specification

Resolving underspecified prompts through agentic clarification before program synthesis, reducing invalid outputs and geometric error.

Open edge: Interaction overhead and generalization beyond the trained text-to-CadQuery trajectory setting; separating clarifier benefit from coder ability.

Shared Direction

  • CAD is usually represented as executable programs or construction sequences rather than raw meshes or B-Rep-only outputs.
  • Geometric fidelity alone is insufficient; validity, executability, and design-intent alignment are increasingly included.
  • LLMs/VLMs and hybrid optimization have become central for CAD generation and reconstruction in the current frontier.
  • Benchmarks highlight a cascade from executable code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design.
  • Method gains are often reported with state-of-the-art claims, but evidence of limitations and reproducibility remains uneven.

Key Differences

  • Generation source differs: text/image-to-CAD versus mesh/point/image-to-CAD reconstruction.
  • Representation and optimization differ: learned end-to-end script generation, hierarchical structure supervision, reinforcement fine-tuning, and IoU-driven iterative fitting.
  • Interaction mode differs: static one-shot generation versus proactive clarification with user/agent dialogue.
  • Evaluation target differs: single-part geometry versus B-Rep assemblies requiring functionality, manufacturability, and assemblability.
  • Model scope differs: task-specific CAD models versus general multimodal LLMs, with specialized models stronger under ideal inputs but brittle across modality shifts.

Open Questions

  • How can text-to-CAD models remain reliable when prompts are ambiguous, noisy, or under-specified without excessive clarification overhead?
  • How should evaluation metrics weight geometric similarity, executability, program compactness, and engineering design intent when rankings change across them?
  • What operation and supervision choices are needed to scale reconstruction/generation to complex mechanical assemblies and manufactured parts?
  • How much of the reported improvement comes from the proposed representation or model versus the surrounding agent, optimization loop, dataset, or benchmark protocol?
  • Which verification gaps in code, datasets, and limitation reporting limit reproducibility across the current CAD methods?

Research Lines

End-to-end multimodal CAD program generation

Converts text, image, sketch, and point-cloud inputs into executable parametric CAD programs using large multimodal models or code-generating pipelines.

Open edge: Robustness under input modality shift and geometric complexity, and engineering validity beyond program executability remain unproven.
Parametric mesh or point-to-CAD reverse engineering

Recovers editable CAD construction sequences from observed geometry through incremental operation fitting and geometric feedback.

Open edge: Invalid program ratios and coverage for complex designs, operation sets, and dataset or kernel transfer are not fully resolved.
Unified CAD benchmarks and evaluation protocols

Provides standardized evaluation across datasets, input modalities, geometric metrics, code executability, and engineering design-intent rubrics.

Open edge: Code and dataset artifact availability plus independent reproduction are verification gaps; judge reliability and metric sensitivity need further evidence.
Agentic grounding and proactive clarification

Aligns natural language specifications to geometric entities or parameters and asks targeted clarification questions before code synthesis to reduce ambiguity.

Open edge: Interaction overhead, kernel generalization, and performance outside curated text-to-CadQuery or grounding datasets are unclear.

Shared Direction

  • CAD generation is increasingly treated as executable program synthesis over procedural or hierarchical construction sequences.
  • Evaluation requires complementary signals spanning geometric fidelity, executability or validity, and engineering design intent such as manufacturability or assemblability.
  • Structure/parameter grounding or stepwise feedback improves consistency and reduces invalid outputs relative to direct sequence prediction.
  • Failures cascade across stages: executable code may still yield invalid geometry or fail engineering requirements.

Key Differences

  • Representation and task scope vary from specialized single-modality CAD generation to unified multimodal multi-task CAD systems.
  • Optimization differs: hybrid geometric feedback/IoU-driven reconstruction versus supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning versus proactive agentic clarification.
  • Grounding mechanism differs: hierarchical structure alignment and parameter perturbation, text-based B-Rep primitive grounding, or stepwise intermediate geometry comparison.
  • Evaluation target differs: geometric fidelity and program validity versus manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability judged by a VLM.
  • Interaction mode differs: proactive clarification before generation versus end-to-end automatic synthesis without disambiguation.

Open Questions

  • Can current systems transfer across CAD kernels, datasets, and infrastructure environments? Supplied evidence lacks independent reproduction artifacts and deployment details.
  • How stable are model rankings under metric choice, input modality shift, and B-rep face-count complexity? Evidence shows rankings change across metrics and specialized models are brittle under modality shift.
  • Can VLM-based design-intent judges reliably substitute for or complement human engineering review? Human validation is mentioned but not fully demonstrated.
  • What is the acceptable trade-off between proactive clarification and automation across real workflows? Evidence shows low interaction overhead in one curated setting, but generalization is unknown.
  • Where exactly do failure cascades begin for complex assemblies: code generation, geometry validity, or design-intent alignment? Current metrics capture stages separately but root causes remain open.

VLM-based CAD code generation and program evolution

Produces editable CadQuery or Python code from images and scales complex training data through iterative program evolution with validation.

Open edge: Real-world image robustness and unseen CAD operations are only partially shown; full operation coverage and open artifact availability remain verification gaps.

Shared Direction

  • Parametric CAD programs are preferred over raw geometry because they provide editable, structured construction sequences.
  • Most systems formulate CAD output as a sequence or program of operations and evaluate generated solids through validity and geometric similarity.
  • Multiple works introduce or use datasets/benchmarks with paired geometry and CAD code or construction sequences to train and compare models.
  • Visual and geometric conditioning is central, but the target is executable or structured CAD rather than rendered pixels alone.
  • Evaluation tends to combine program validity with geometric metrics such as IoU, Chamfer distance, solid similarity, or executable B-rep assertions.

Key Differences

  • Input representation differs: some methods consume meshes or point clouds, others consume images, sketches, text, or stepwise orthographic views.
  • Generation paradigm differs: hybrid optimization, offline reinforcement learning with geometric rewards, state-space latent GAN, VLM fine-tuning, and VLM-guided program evolution are used.
  • Supervision signal differs: geometric feedback and IoU rewards, paired image-code supervision, self-supervised reconstruction, and executable assertion scoring reflect different training objectives.
  • Evaluation target differs: some compare benchmark-specific reconstruction accuracy, others measure valid syntax and 3D similarity, while diagnostic work uses causal controls and independent geometric assertions.
  • Interaction mode differs: end-to-end generation in one model, stepwise feature-by-feature construction, and iterative program evolution with validation imply different CAD workflow assumptions.

Open Questions

  • Can reconstruction and generation models maintain validity and geometric accuracy on real-world industrial parts and unseen CAD operations beyond curated benchmarks?
  • How can the field avoid circular evaluation where extracted headers or geometric reprojections share dependencies with the model's training objective?
  • Which supervision or generation paradigm scales best to long sequences, complex features such as fillets and chamfers, and low invalid-program rates?
  • Do unified multimodal multi-task CAD models sacrifice task-specific performance or fail on out-of-benchmark applied CAD workflow steps?
  • Given missing code, dataset, and limitation details for several top papers, can reported state-of-the-art results be independently reproduced and audited?

Research Lines

Benchmark-driven evaluation of programmatic CAD generation (CADBench, MUSE, BenchCAD)

Provides stratified multimodal samples and staged metrics to compare generators on geometry, executability, code QA/editing, and engineering intent.

Open edge: Unseen part family generalization is limited; metric rankings shift; VLM-judged design intent has limited human or physical validation; several artifacts are not clearly reproducible.
Text-to-CAD program synthesis with grounding and alignment (FutureCAD, HierCAD, ProCAD)

Converts natural language specifications into executable CadQuery or parametric construction trees, using primitive grounding, structure alignment, or clarification to reduce invalid outputs and ambiguous specification errors.

Open edge: Fine-grained parameters and complex operations are still misinferred or simplified; clarification interaction overhead and broader coverage of design specifications are not fully evidenced.
Geometry-native generative representations for CAD (DreamCAD, Mamba-CAD)

Learns latent or parametric surface representations, enabling generation from point-level or self-supervised signals and longer CAD sequences without dense CAD-specific paired annotations.

Open edge: Brittleness under modality shift and geometric complexity is observed; engineering constraints and real-world editability remain mostly unevaluated; code or dataset availability is limited.
Formal design constraints and evaluation rubrics for CAD generation

Defines code check, geometric check, and design-intent rubrics for manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability, making engineering-oriented requirements measurable beyond geometric similarity.

Open edge: Whether rubrics correspond to formal engineering validity or manufacturing outcomes is not established; benchmark coverage and judge reliability need further validation across unseen industrial diversity.

Shared Direction

  • Executable CAD programs or editable B-Reps are preferred targets over raw meshes because they support downstream engineering manipulation and constraint checking.
  • Staged evaluation is necessary because models often produce executable code but fail valid geometry or design intent, a recurring failure cascade.
  • Specialized geometric grounding, hierarchical decomposition, or clarification improves robustness over direct end-to-end generation in the reported evidence.
  • No single metric or dataset family is sufficient; rankings are sensitive to modality, part complexity, and evaluation rubric.

Key Differences

  • Representation choices differ: script-based CadQuery, hierarchical construction trees, parametric Bézier surfaces, and state-space latent sequences trade editability, training signal, and sequence capacity differently.
  • Supervision strategies differ: curated text-to-CAD pairs, point-level unannotated 3D data, self-supervised reconstruction, and reinforcement learning are used, implying different assumptions about available annotations.
  • Interaction mode differs: some systems generate directly, some ground structure and parameters internally, and some ask clarification questions before generation.
  • Evaluation targets differ: geometric fidelity and executability are measured in some benchmarks, while others emphasize code QA, code editing, manufacturability, functionality, or assemblability.
  • Deployment settings differ: idealized mesh or image inputs versus industrial part families and complex assemblies, which changes generalization expectations.

Open Questions

  • How reliably do VLM-judged engineering rubrics predict real manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability?
  • Under what input shifts and unseen part families can specialized CAD generators maintain their advantage over general LLMs?
  • Which representation and supervision combination best balances editability, geometric fidelity, and training from unannotated or weakly supervised data?
  • Can parameter grounding and proactive clarification reduce fine-grained parameter substitution errors beyond the evaluated datasets?
  • What is missing from the current evidence base: several top papers do not surface code, dataset, baseline, metrics, or limitations, so reproducibility and failure boundaries remain verification gaps.

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
