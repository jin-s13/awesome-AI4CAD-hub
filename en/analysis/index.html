Research Lines

Unified multimodal CAD benchmarks and evaluation protocols

Providing controlled sample stratification, input modalities, and metrics for comparing CAD program generation across geometric complexity and task settings.

Open edge: Whether benchmark rankings and failure modes transfer to noisy real-world engineering workflows; missing code, dataset, or artifact signals leave verification gaps.
LLM-based text-to-CAD program generation with grounded parameter and structure reasoning

Converting text prompts into executable parametric CAD programs with better topology and parameter consistency.

Open edge: Evidence mostly reports aggregate state-of-the-art; robustness to ambiguous specifications, real-world text diversity, and the isolated contribution of grounding or reinforcement-learning modules remain unclear.
Mesh/image-to-CAD reverse engineering by stepwise or optimization-based reconstruction

Recovering editable parametric CAD operations and construction sequences from meshes or images with improved geometric fidelity and validity.

Open edge: Scalability beyond evaluated part families, sensitivity to noisy or photorealistic modalities, and how intermediate geometric comparison generalizes remain unresolved.
Universal multimodal multi-task CAD models

Handling point-to-CAD reconstruction, text/image-to-CAD generation, and CAD question answering in one end-to-end framework.

Open edge: Cross-task transfer and whether single-model generality loses accuracy against specialized mesh-to-CAD under complex geometry are not established.

Shared Direction

  • The target representation is an executable parametric CAD program or construction sequence rather than only a mesh or point cloud.
  • Evaluation commonly includes geometric fidelity and program validity or executability, with increasing use of multimodal inputs.
  • Specialized CAD reconstruction methods and general LLM/VLM-based methods are compared directly, revealing that model ranking can change with metric and modality.
  • There is a recognized failure cascade from executable code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target: geometric fidelity metrics versus executability or invalid ratio versus manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • Supervision: supervised fine-tuning plus reinforcement learning versus structure-aligned and parameter-grounded training versus optimization-based fitting.
  • Generation strategy: direct script generation, hierarchical trajectory reasoning, stepwise feature construction, or proactive clarification before synthesis.
  • Model scope: task-specific CAD models versus a single multimodal multi-task model, with trade-offs between reliability and generalization.
  • Interaction mode: single-turn generation from a complete prompt versus agentic clarification that asks follow-up questions.

Open Questions

  • Which evaluation criteria, beyond geometric similarity and executability, best predict manufacturable and assemblable CAD?
  • Can unified multimodal models match task-specific mesh-to-CAD models on complex B-rep geometry and under noisy or photorealistic inputs?
  • How well do proactive clarification and grounding strategies transfer beyond curated CAD datasets to real-world design specifications?
  • What part of text-to-CAD gains comes from the LLM, grounding module, training data, or reinforcement-learning stage rather than from the surrounding system?
  • Are benchmark rankings stable across geometric complexity, input modality, and metric definitions?

Assesses beyond geometry: manufacturability, functionality, assemblability, and failure cascades from code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design.

Open edge: Validity and calibration of rubric-based VLM judges and coverage of real assembly and tolerance constraints.
Structured parametric CAD generation and reconstruction baselines

Provides methods that output executable CAD programs or construction sequences, enabling measurable validity and reconstruction quality.

Open edge: Generalization from idealized inputs to complex, noisy, or shifted modalities, and stable ranking across metrics.
Interactive clarification and agentic text-to-CAD evaluation

Reduces ambiguity and invalid outputs by clarifying prompts before program synthesis, with low interaction overhead.

Open edge: Whether clarification protocols reflect real user workflows and scale beyond evaluated prompts.

Shared Direction

  • CAD generation should be evaluated as executable structured programs rather than only as final meshes.
  • Aggregate metrics such as validity, geometric fidelity, compactness, and invalid ratio appear across studies but are not sufficient alone.
  • Recent evidence converges on multimodal evaluation and on separating idealized geometry from engineering-ready design intent.
  • Model rankings are sensitive to metric and modality choices, supporting multi-metric stress-test evaluations.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target differs: some work evaluates geometric reconstruction quality, while others evaluate manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • Interaction mode differs: end-to-end generation without clarification versus proactive clarification before code synthesis.
  • Representation and optimization differ: construction-tree or CadQuery generation with supervised or reinforcement signals versus optimization-driven parametric reconstruction using geometric feedback.
  • Judging mechanism differs: geometric metrics compared against VLM rubric judges, with limited evidence on reliability and human alignment.

Open Questions

  • Do benchmark task distributions cover real CAD settings that include tolerance, assembly context, and manufacturing constraints?
  • Do rubric-based VLM judges align with practicing engineers and remain consistent across design specifications?
  • Are benchmark conclusions stable across implementations, compute budgets, and dataset sampling?
  • What interaction budget and clarification protocol best improves text-to-CAD robustness without burdening users?
  • Which missing artifacts and limitation details in the evidence packet create verification gaps for reproducibility and auditability?

Unified multimodal and multi-task CAD platforms and benchmarks

Evaluating heterogeneous CAD tasks across text, image, sketch, mesh, and point cloud inputs, with executable program generation and geometric or engineering checks.

Open edge: Generalization to external CAD tools and deployment settings, independent reproduction, and avoiding metric or judge failure cascades.
Agentic and grounded text-to-CAD program generation

Turning natural-language design intent into executable parametric CAD scripts through hierarchical structure alignment, parameter grounding, and proactive clarification.

Open edge: Interaction overhead versus output quality, coverage of real-world CAD corpora, and robustness beyond curated prompts.
Geometry-to-CAD reconstruction and reverse engineering pipelines

Recovering editable parametric CAD construction sequences from meshes or point clouds through incremental optimization or stepwise construction.

Open edge: Scaling to complex assemblies, noisy or shifted modalities, and validity beyond geometric metrics.
Engineering-readiness evaluation and design-intent alignment

Assessing manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability beyond geometric similarity.

Open edge: VLM-judge reliability, limited human validation, and missing lifecycle or deployment evidence.

Shared Direction

  • Executable parametric CAD programs are favored as an intermediate representation because they support editability and validity checking.
  • Evaluation is moving beyond shape similarity toward geometric fidelity, executability, program compactness, and engineering intent.
  • Recent systems increasingly use LLMs, VLMs, or learned agents to generate structured CAD code with grounding or feedback loops.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target differs: some work prioritizes geometric reconstruction accuracy, while others target design-intent alignment, manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • Input supervision differs: text, images, sketches, point clouds, and meshes are treated as separate modalities in some systems and unified in others.
  • Interaction mode differs: some systems generate in a single pass, while others use proactive clarification agents or stepwise iterative reconstruction.
  • Optimization and supervision differ: approaches include supervised fine-tuning with reinforcement learning, ranking-based parameter grounding, IoU-driven optimization, and agentic supervised fine-tuning.

Shared Direction

  • CAD is represented as parametric programs or construction sequences, with editable operations as the target rather than plain shape output.
  • Geometric consistency and program validity or syntax are shared evaluation concerns across recent methods.
  • Paired data between non-CAD inputs and programs or code is central to training recent models.
  • DeepCAD, Fusion 360, and MCB benchmarks recur as common evaluation settings.

Key Differences

  • Input coverage differs: some methods reconstruct from meshes or point clouds, while others condition on images, sketches, or text.
  • Optimization and supervision differ: hybrid geometric optimization, offline reinforcement learning, VLM fine-tuning, program evolution, and state-space or GAN-based generation are all present.
  • Program representation differs between native parametric operation sequences and executable CadQuery Python code.
  • Evaluation differs between surface-similarity or validity metrics and executable geometric assertions used for diagnostic contrast.
  • Generalization claims differ: some works report state-of-the-art benchmark scores, while one evidence point shows only partial real-world and unseen-operation generalization.

  • Are current geometric-similarity metrics circular or biased toward surface appearance, and do executable assertions align with engineering validity?

Research Lines

Multimodal benchmarks for AI-assisted CAD program generation and evaluation

Standardizes evaluation across text, image, and 3D inputs; separates code executability, geometric validity, and design-intent or engineering readiness.

Open edge: Generalization to unseen part families and real manufacturing workflows, plus reliable automated design-intent assessment.
LLM-driven text-to-CAD program generation with structural grounding

Translates natural language design specifications into executable parametric CAD scripts using hierarchy, grounding, or specification clarification.

Open edge: Stable handling of fine-grained engineering constraints, complex assemblies, and generalization beyond curated training distributions.
Latent or sequence generative modeling for parametric CAD

Learns self-supervised latent representations and generates long parametric CAD sequences without relying only on language supervision.

Open edge: Whether long-sequence validity transfers to complex topology, editability, and distribution shift under modality or geometric complexity changes.
Differentiable parametric surface and B-Rep representation learning

Produces editable B-Rep from point-level supervision without CAD-specific annotations, supporting text, image, and point modalities.

Open edge: Scaling to assemblies and engineering constraints beyond geometry-oriented benchmarks such as ABC and Objaverse.

Shared Direction

  • The field distinguishes code executability, geometric validity, and design-intent or engineering readiness; success at one stage does not guarantee downstream success.
  • Editable B-Rep or parametric CAD is the shared target representation, rather than only mesh or point-cloud output.
  • Multiple metrics are necessary because model rankings change across geometric, executability, and compactness-style measures.
  • Models perform better under idealized or curated inputs and become brittle under complex geometry or modality shift.

Key Differences

  • Representation and supervision differ: differentiable parametric patches, LLM-generated scripts, and latent sequence models use different training signals and assumptions.
  • Evaluation targets differ: some work prioritizes geometric fidelity and executability, while other work targets manufacturability, assemblability, design-intent alignment, or industrial code editing.
  • Interaction mode differs: some approaches generate in a single pass, while others introduce proactive clarification before code synthesis.
  • Data setting differs: curated text-to-CAD datasets, large unannotated 3D data, and industrial part-family program collections create different generalization conditions.

Open Questions

  • Can current text-to-CAD methods generalize to unseen part families and real manufacturing constraints rather than only improving in-distribution performance?
  • When is proactive clarification necessary, and how can ambiguity be resolved with low interaction overhead?
  • Can differentiable B-Rep representations scale to complex assemblies and arbitrary topology while remaining editable?
  • When multiple metrics conflict, which metrics predict real engineering utility?

Dataset creation for manufacturing processes

Providing a multimodal dataset (Slice-100K) for extrusion-based 3D printing, which can facilitate learning-based slicing and toolpath planning.

Open edge: The downstream model performance and the dataset's coverage of manufacturing variability are not discussed in the evidence.

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
