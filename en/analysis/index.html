Shared Direction

  • High-visibility work treats CAD as executable program or parametric sequence generation rather than unstructured geometry regression.
  • Evaluation is moving toward multimodal, multi-metric benchmarks that jointly report geometric fidelity, executability, and design-intent or engineering criteria.
  • Several methods decompose generation into symbolic reasoning, primitive or parameter grounding, and code or operation synthesis instead of one-shot output.
  • Benchmarks separate specialized CAD models from general-purpose multimodal LLMs; specialized models can be stronger under idealized inputs, but rankings and robustness vary by modality and metric.
  • Invalidity or executability ratios are reported alongside geometric scores, showing dual concern for syntactic validity and geometric quality.

Key Differences

  • Representation: CadQuery/script generation, B-Rep primitive grounding, construction-tree or stepwise operations, and unified multimodal tokenization.
  • Supervision: supervised fine-tuning plus reinforcement learning, ranking-based structure/parameter supervision, hybrid geometric optimization, and agent-generated clarification trajectories.
  • Evaluation target: Chamfer/IoU geometric fidelity, executability or invalid ratio, or manufacturability/functionality/assemblability rubrics.
  • Task scope: some approaches unify text/image/point-to-CAD and CAD question answering in one model, while others specialize on one input interface.
  • Interaction mode: proactive clarification agents intervene before synthesis, while non-agent pipelines assume complete inputs and directly optimize.

Open Questions

  • Which method contributions transfer across reported benchmarks when input modality, object complexity, and metric weighting shift?
  • How much of reported gain comes from curated datasets, benchmark construction, or base-model capability rather than the proposed representation or objective?
  • What is the limiting failure in the cascade from executable code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design, and how should it be measured?
  • Do unified multi-task CAD models retain specialized reconstruction fidelity and robustness under distribution shift?
  • How reliable are automatic VLM-judge rubrics for engineering criteria compared with human review outside limited validation sets?

Metric and stress-test design for CAD outputs

Separates executable validity, geometric fidelity, and design-intent criteria, while stress-testing robustness to ambiguous prompts and complex geometry.

Open edge: Alignment between proxy metrics such as IoU, Chamfer distance, invalid ratio, or VLM judging and downstream manufacturability, assemblability, and user decisions.
Comparable baselines and reporting for CAD program synthesis

Provides shared baselines, multi-metric rankings, and validity reporting so that specialized and general models can be audited on the same outputs.

Open edge: Whether benchmark conclusions remain stable across implementations and compute budgets, and whether missing limitation and artifact details limit auditability.

Shared Direction

  • CAD benchmarks treat outputs as executable programs and evaluate both program validity and resulting geometry.
  • Multimodal conditioning is a shared evaluation setting, spanning text, images, meshes, point clouds, and sketches.
  • Multi-metric evaluation is necessary because model rankings change across geometric, validity, and design-oriented metrics.
  • Benchmarks commonly measure reconstruction quality and invalid program ratio, emphasizing editable parametric B-Rep or CAD sequence recovery.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target differs: some work emphasizes geometric fidelity and executability, while others assess manufacturability, functionality, assemblability, and design-intent alignment.
  • Task scope differs: some benchmarks target a single task such as mesh-to-CAD or text-to-CAD, while others unify multiple tasks and modalities in one suite.
  • Interaction mode differs: some systems are end-to-end generators, while others use proactive clarification before CAD code synthesis.
  • Generation and supervision mechanisms differ: LLM script generation, structure and parameter grounding, hybrid optimization, and stepwise reverse engineering each address CAD reconstruction differently.
  • CAD representation differs: CadQuery scripts, hierarchical construction trees, parametric sequences, and B-Rep assemblies are evaluated under different assumptions.

Open Questions

  • Do multi-metric benchmark rankings remain stable across different implementations, compute budgets, and dataset samples?
  • Which proxy metrics validly reflect engineering decisions such as manufacturability, assemblability, and design intent?
  • How well do current CAD benchmarks cover ambiguous prompts, modality shift, and complex geometric settings?
  • The packet leaves limitation fields empty for many top works and lacks consistent code/dataset/artifact evidence, leaving generalizability and failure boundaries unverified.

Research Lines

Unified multimodal CAD program generation and benchmarking

Standardizes evaluation of CAD program generation across image, mesh, point cloud, and text inputs; measures geometric fidelity, executability, compactness, and engineering design criteria.

Open edge: Real-world design intent, manufacturing and assembly constraints, cross-dataset generalization, and judge or metric reliability are not yet established; many artifacts lack verified independent reproduction.
Agentic and LLM-driven text-to-CAD pipelines

Converts ambiguous natural language specifications into executable parametric CAD scripts, using clarification, structure alignment, primitive grounding, or instruction tuning to improve validity.

Open edge: Interaction overhead, robustness to complex assemblies, and the boundary where clarification is sufficient remain unresolved; deployed reliability beyond curated prompts is a verification gap.
Geometry-to-CAD reverse engineering and reconstruction

Recovers editable parametric CAD construction sequences and operations from meshes, images, or point clouds using iterative fitting, optimization, or stepwise geometry comparison.

Open edge: Trade-offs among speed, geometric fidelity, program validity, and complexity remain open; performance under noisy input, hardware variation, and external datasets is incompletely evidenced.
Reproducible CAD evaluation artifacts and engineering systems

Provides benchmarks, datasets, code-visible signals, and evaluation protocols so CAD systems can be inspected, compared, and potentially reused in downstream pipelines.

Open edge: Independent reproduction across teams, infrastructure, and downstream engineering workflows is not shown consistently; missing limitation statements and artifact details leave verification gaps.

Shared Direction

  • CAD generation is increasingly framed as producing executable structured programs or construction sequences rather than only surface meshes.
  • Evaluation is moving toward multi-metric benchmarks that combine geometric fidelity with program executability or validity, and sometimes design-intent criteria.
  • Many systems share grounding and validation mechanisms, using alignment, perturbation, optimization, or geometric feedback to improve parameter and topology consistency.
  • Current systems consistently reveal a gap between producing valid CAD code and satisfying fine-grained engineering, manufacturing, or assembly requirements.

Key Differences

  • Input assumptions differ: some systems target clean mesh or image reconstruction, while others handle ambiguous text prompts or mixed multimodal inputs.
  • Supervision and optimization strategies differ: supervised or reinforcement-tuned LLM code generation, hierarchical trajectory alignment, hybrid geometric optimization, and proactive clarification each have distinct trade-offs.
  • Evaluation targets split between geometric or surface recovery, program validity and compactness, and design-level criteria such as manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • Interaction mode differs: one-pass generation coexists with agentic clarification-and-coding loops, implying different assumptions about human-in-the-loop effort.

Open Questions

  • Can CAD systems maintain validity and design intent under ambiguous user specifications and complex assemblies beyond curated benchmark conditions?
  • What practical latency, compute, and reliability trade-offs arise in interactive or deployed CAD pipelines?
  • How robust are current evaluation judges and curated benchmarks to independent reproduction and to real manufacturing and assembly constraints?
  • Which grounding or optimization strategy transfers across input modalities, datasets, and downstream CAD kernels without brittle parameter assumptions?

Shared Direction

  • Structured CAD operations and stepwise or iterative geometric feedback are consistently used to improve validity over one-shot prediction.
  • Evaluation relies heavily on geometric similarity and program validity, with emerging attention to executable assertions and design-intent adherence.
  • Large task-specific datasets, unified benchmarks, and multimodal models are being adopted to scale CAD generation and reconstruction.
  • Reproducibility evidence is uneven; several high-ranked papers in the packet lack visible limitation or artifact details.

Key Differences

  • They differ in input and target representation: meshes, images, point clouds, orthographic views, textual design-intent headers, and executable CAD code.
  • Supervision and generation mechanisms differ: hybrid optimization, offline reinforcement learning, state-space latent generation, VLM fine-tuning, and evolutionary program growth.
  • Evaluation emphasis differs: volumetric IoU and Chamfer distance, valid syntax rate, invalid ratio, executable geometric assertions, and design-intent adherence are used with different priorities.
  • Some systems operate end-to-end in a single model, while others decompose reconstruction into stepwise features, prefixes, or program evolution.
  • Deployment assumptions differ: several methods report strong benchmark gains, while only some test real-world images, unseen operations, or conditioning failures.

LLM and agentic text-to-CAD program synthesis

Translating natural language or clarified specifications into executable CAD programs and grounding language to B-Rep primitives.

Open edge: Fine-grained engineering criteria remain hard; ambiguity handling and code synthesis rely on curated high-quality datasets and interaction overhead.
Parametric, B-Rep, and state-space generative modeling

Learning generative mappings from text, image, point clouds, or latent representations to editable parametric CAD/B-Rep outputs, including longer sequences.

Open edge: Long-sequence scalability, point-supervision without CAD annotations, and robustness under modality shift are not yet fully validated.
Engineering-oriented design-intent evaluation

Assessing manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability via staged code, geometry, and design-intent alignment checks.

Open edge: Whether automated VLM-judge rubrics match human engineering judgment and whether they change model design choices.

Shared Direction

  • Executable programmatic CAD and editable B-Rep outputs are treated as a shared interface for studying generative CAD.
  • Evaluation must be multidimensional because models are ranked differently by geometric fidelity, executability, and program compactness.
  • Frontier models often recover coarse outer geometry but miss fine 3D structure, parameter semantics, and complex operations such as sweeps and lofts.
  • Fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, or curation improves in-distribution performance but leaves out-of-distribution part-family generalization limited.

Key Differences

  • Approach: program synthesis via LLMs versus state-space/latent generative modeling versus differentiable parametric surface fitting.
  • Supervision: curated CAD code and reinforcement/ranking signals versus point-level supervision on unannotated 3D data.
  • Evaluation target: idealized geometric reconstruction versus multimodal robustness versus downstream design-intent engineering properties.
  • Interaction: proactive clarification before generation versus direct generation from possibly ambiguous prompts.

Open Questions

  • Do results from current CAD benchmarks predict performance on unseen industrial part families and real engineering workflows?
  • Can formal geometric constraint checks replace or supplement VLM-based design-intent rubrics?
  • Why do model rankings change across metrics, and which evaluation setting should govern deployment?
  • How can long-sequence parametric CAD generation scale without scarce execution-verified and CAD-annotated data?

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
