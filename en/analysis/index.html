Research Lines

Parametric program synthesis and multimodal CAD generation

Converting text, image, sketch, or point cloud inputs into editable, executable CAD programs and grounding language to B-Rep primitives or topology/parameter sequences.

Open edge: Cross-modal transfer and robustness under input shift; exact attribution to representation, fine-tuning, RL, or agentic prompting; missing code, data, and limitation evidence for reproduction.
Geometry-aware reconstruction and optimization

Recovering CAD construction sequences from meshes or images through iterative parametric fitting, hybrid optimization, or stepwise geometric comparison while preserving editability and validity.

Open edge: Rich operation coverage and complex geometry; separating optimization gain from model contribution versus task prior; long-horizon and large assembly evidence is limited.
Benchmark-driven evaluation for CAD methods

Controlled evaluation across modalities, geometric complexity, validity, and engineering criteria such as manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.

Open edge: Metric stability and whether automated judges align with human design intent; model rankings shift across metrics; B-rep and STEP family coverage remains partial.
Interactive and agentic text-to-CAD workflows

Resolving ambiguous specifications through targeted clarification before code synthesis and reducing invalid outputs while controlling interaction overhead.

Open edge: Scaling to multi-part assemblies; transfer of clarification policy across base coding models; trade-off between dialogue overhead and generation quality is not fully measured.

Shared Direction

  • Current methods concentrate on producing parametric CAD programs or construction sequences because programmatic output enables editability, compactness, and executability.
  • LLM-based program synthesis is becoming a common substrate across text, image, sketch, and point inputs, while geometry-specialized models retain advantages for idealized mesh reconstruction.
  • Evaluation commonly includes executability/validity and geometric fidelity such as IoU or Chamfer distance, but newer work adds engineering intent rubrics; model rankings are metric- and modality-sensitive.
  • Missing code, dataset, metric, baseline, and limitation details in the packet are verification gaps rather than evidence that methods lack reproducibility.

Key Differences

  • Model and input focus differs: some methods rely on CAD-specialized reconstruction, others on general LLM/VLM code generation, and others on a single universal multimodal model; evidence suggests specialization helps idealized inputs but is brittle under modality shift.
  • Generation strategy differs: direct autoregressive program synthesis, hierarchical decomposition with parameter grounding, hybrid optimization, stepwise geometric feedback, and proactive clarification place corrective signals at different stages.
  • Evaluation target differs: geometric fidelity and executability are central in some work, while others foreground manufacturability, functionality, assemblability, or multi-task question answering, leading to different conclusions about model quality.
  • Supervision and interaction differ: supervised fine-tuning plus RL, ranking-based parameter supervision, IoU-driven optimization, and agentic clarification are used, so reported gains may mix model, optimization loop, and prompt or agent contributions.

Open Questions

  • Can a universal model match task-specific reconstruction or generation across clean/noisy meshes, single/multi-view, text, and sketches without modality brittleness?
  • How should CAD benchmarks weight geometric fidelity, executability, compactness, and engineering criteria such as manufacturability or assemblability, and do automated judges align with expert design intent?
  • Which component contributes in hybrid optimization, agentic clarification, or unified MLLM pipelines: the model, the planner or optimization loop, the dataset, or the interaction policy?
  • What remains uncovered for complex B-Rep assemblies, rich operations, and long-horizon design tasks, given current evidence focuses heavily on single-object or benchmark-sample generation?
  • Editing and retrieval method evidence is sparse in the bounded packet; the current frontier is concentrated on generation, reconstruction, and CAD question answering, leaving editing and retrieval-specific contributions unverified.
  • To what extent do absent code, dataset, metric, baseline, and limitation details limit reproducibility and cross-method comparison in this topic?

Text-to-CAD program generation and grounding

Generates executable parametric CAD scripts from natural language, using structure alignment, parameter grounding, or clarification to improve validity and fidelity.

Open edge: Engineering constraints beyond geometric validity, such as manufacturability and design intent, are only partially evaluated; ambiguity handling requires interaction overhead and curated data.
Mesh-to-CAD reverse engineering

Recovers editable parametric CAD construction sequences from meshes via stepwise fitting or hybrid optimization, improving volumetric fidelity and reducing invalid programs.

Open edge: Evidence shows degradation with geometric complexity and brittleness under input modality shift; program compactness and generalization beyond existing datasets remain open.
Engineering-oriented evaluation and automated judging

Adds staged code, geometry, and design-intent checks for B-Rep assemblies, including manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability via VLM judges.

Open edge: Automated judge reliability is only partially validated; fine-grained engineering criteria and real manufacturing constraints are not yet shown to align with downstream use.

Shared Direction

  • Executable parametric CAD programs are the dominant target representation across generation, reconstruction, and evaluation pipelines.
  • A single aggregate metric is insufficient; evaluation increasingly combines geometric fidelity, executability or validity, compactness, and engineering-intent criteria.
  • CAD-specialized pipelines can outperform general vision-language models under controlled inputs, but success does not guarantee robust behavior under complexity growth or modality shift.
  • Benchmarks reveal a failure cascade from executable code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design, making staged evaluation necessary.

Key Differences

  • Input representation and task scope differ: some work evaluates multimodal reconstruction and generation together, while others focus on text-to-CAD or mesh-to-CAD only.
  • Optimization and supervision differ: LLM fine-tuning with reinforcement learning, ranking-based structure/parameter grounding, hybrid IoU-driven optimization, and stepwise intermediate geometry comparison each encode different inductive biases.
  • Evaluation target differs: geometric fidelity and program validity are common, but engineering-focused evaluation adds manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability of assemblies rather than only single parts.
  • Interaction mode differs: one-shot generation is standard, but proactive specification clarification introduces human-in-the-loop cost to resolve ambiguous prompts.

Open Questions

  • How well do proxy metrics align with downstream decisions engineers actually care about across manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability?
  • Why do model rankings change across metrics and input modality shifts, and what evaluation protocol would make them stable?
  • Do current CAD sample distributions adequately cover real-world complexity, ambiguity, and assembly workflows?
  • How can automated VLM judges be validated beyond limited human checks for fine-grained design-intent criteria?
  • Are reported conclusions stable across implementations, code availability, and compute budgets when several papers lack visible artifact or limitation details?

Converting natural-language design intents into executable parametric CAD scripts, with structure grounding, parameter grounding, hierarchical reasoning, or proactive clarification.

Open edge: Fine-grained engineering success remains limited; ambiguity handling adds interaction overhead; real-world deployment robustness beyond reported datasets is unclear.
Mesh-to-CAD reverse engineering via stepwise and optimization-based reconstruction

Recovering editable parametric CAD construction sequences from meshes by incremental operation fitting and validation.

Open edge: Evidence is mostly on part-level reconstruction; assembly-level, deployment, and independent cross-benchmark reproduction remain open.
Engineering-readiness evaluation frameworks

Evaluating generated CAD beyond geometry: executability, manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability through staged code, geometry, and design-intent checks.

Open edge: VLM judge reliability and human alignment need more validation; the failure cascade from code to engineering-ready design remains unresolved.

Shared Direction

  • Work in this packet converges on parametric CAD program generation or reconstruction as the central system output, rather than producing only meshes or images.
  • There is shared recognition that generation quality must be decomposed into multiple checks: syntactic executability, geometric validity, and design-intent or engineering fitness.
  • Recent systems increasingly combine large language or vision-language models with CAD-specific structure, parameter grounding, or optimization to improve semantic control.
  • Across benchmarks and methods, authors report a common failure cascade: executable code often does not produce valid geometry, and valid geometry often does not satisfy engineering-ready criteria.

Key Differences

  • Systems differ in supervision and representation: some use training-time structure/parameter alignment or reinforcement, while others use inference-time hybrid optimization or stepwise geometric comparison.
  • Task coverage differs: some systems emphasize unified multimodal multi-task generation, while others focus on a narrower input-to-CAD conversion or interactive text-to-CAD clarification.
  • Evaluation goals differ: one group prioritizes geometric fidelity, executability, and program compactness across input modalities; another prioritizes manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability through design-intent rubrics.
  • Interaction mode differs: some systems operate as one-shot generators, while others introduce a proactive clarification agent that asks targeted questions before code synthesis.
  • Deployment assumptions differ: some assume idealized input geometry or curated datasets, while others target real-world ambiguous prompts and practical design instances.

Open Questions

  • What independent reproduction evidence would show that reported gains hold across datasets, hardware, and evaluation implementations?
  • How can systems overcome the failure cascade from executable code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design without overfitting to a single benchmark metric?
  • What is the right balance between proactive clarification overhead and robustness for real-world design workflows?
  • How do unified multi-task models compare with specialized reconstruction pipelines under noisy, multi-view, or geometrically complex inputs?
  • How reliable are VLM-based judges for manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability when compared with human engineering review?

Generating executable, editable CAD code directly from visual inputs, with high syntax validity and 3D similarity.

Open edge: Robustness beyond training distribution is only partially shown; real-world images and unseen operations lack full evidence.

Turning CAD reconstruction into sequential decisions informed by intermediate orthographic projections and alignment rewards.

Open edge: Data-efficiency and transfer across input noise, datasets, and actual design workflows remain open.

Shared Direction

  • Parametric CAD programs are preferred over mesh-only outputs because they preserve editability and structured operations.
  • Geometric feedback and stepwise construction are common mechanisms for improving reconstruction quality and validity.
  • Evaluation combines syntactic or executable validity with geometric similarity metrics such as IoU, Chamfer Distance, or B-rep assertions.
  • Recent methods increasingly use multimodal models or large-language/vision-language models for heterogeneous CAD tasks.
  • Synthetic or augmented program-geometry datasets are used to address training data constraints.

Key Differences

  • Input representation varies across mesh, point cloud, image, sketch, and orthographic projections.
  • Supervision signal differs: direct geometric optimization, stepwise view rewards, RL-based sequence decisions, program evolution, or design-intent headers.
  • Model choices differ from optimization over structured programs, state-space plus GAN generation, and offline Decision Transformer to VLM fine-tuning.
  • Evaluation targets differ: validity ratio and geometric distance, 100% syntax validity, cross-benchmark state-of-the-art, B-rep geometric assertions, or data efficiency.
  • Scope ranges from single-task mesh/image-to-CAD reconstruction to unified multi-task systems and diagnostic causal tests of model behavior.

Open Questions

  • Do mesh/image-to-CAD gains transfer across DeepCAD, Fusion 360, MCB, CADENA-Bench, and real-world industrial parts?
  • Can unified or VLM-based CAD models preserve semantic design intent and editability rather than only geometric similarity?
  • How can benchmarks avoid circularity between header extraction, program validity, and geometric evaluation?
  • How do state-space, VLM, and program-evolution methods scale to longer sequences, complex operations, and constrained industrial design?
  • Missing code, datasets, limitations, and citation edges leave reproducibility and comparative impact as verification gaps.

Research Lines

Formal representation and constraint analysis

Clarifies what executable, manufacturable, functional, and assemblable CAD representations must satisfy by operationalizing staged code, geometry, and design-intent checks.

Open edge: Formal theory linking assumptions to CAD validity is sparse; the evidence mainly offers empirical rubrics rather than proofs of constraint satisfaction.
Executable parametric CAD program generation

Translates text, image, or point inputs into CadQuery scripts or parametric CAD sequences that can be executed and edited.

Open edge: Models often recover coarse outer geometry but miss fine 3D structure and substitute complex sweep or loft operations with simpler sketch-and-extrude patterns.
B-Rep and parametric surface grounding

Enables editable B-Rep generation from weak point-level supervision or natural-language primitive grounding without dense CAD annotations.

Open edge: Brittleness under modality shift and geometric complexity, plus limited evidence that grounded primitives enforce manufacturability constraints.
Benchmarking and staged design-intent evaluation

Measures code validity, geometry fidelity, and engineering attributes such as manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability across many samples and tasks.

Open edge: Metric rankings can change across evaluation criteria; benchmarks only partially connect generated geometry to formal design constraints or real fabrication readiness.

Shared Direction

  • The field is converging on executable parametric CAD programs or editable B-Reps as a shared intermediate representation for generation.
  • Multi-stage evaluation from code validity to geometry to design intent reveals a failure cascade: many outputs are syntactically valid but not engineering-ready.
  • Frontier LLMs and VLMs capture coarse geometry but struggle with fine parametric detail, while specialized models excel under idealized inputs but degrade under modality shift.
  • Benchmarks increasingly include industrial-scale, execution-verified samples and multimodal inputs.

Key Differences

  • Representation: CadQuery or parametric sequence scripts versus B-Rep parametric surfaces or patches.
  • Supervision: paired text-to-CAD or image-to-CAD data versus point-level weak supervision versus clarification trajectories.
  • Evaluation target: geometric fidelity and executability versus manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • Interaction mode: one-shot generation versus proactive clarification before synthesis.
  • Architecture: LLM-or-VLM code generators versus state-space or GAN latent generators versus differentiable parametric surface generators.

Open Questions

  • Can models generalize to unseen industrial part families after fine-tuning or reinforcement learning?
  • What formal constraints or design rules would guarantee manufacturable and assemblable B-Rep outputs rather than only empirical rubrics?
  • How should evaluation metrics be weighted when model rankings change across geometry, compactness, and engineering-readiness criteria?
  • Does proactive clarification remain low-overhead and complete for complex real-world CAD specifications?
  • Are the reproducibility signals sufficient to validate robustness claims, given missing limitation statements and varied artifact availability in the packet?

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
