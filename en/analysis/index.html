Research Lines

LLM-driven parametric CAD program generation with structure and primitive grounding

Converts text, image, sketch, or multimodal prompts into executable parametric CAD scripts or grounded B-Rep primitives, improving structure consistency and parameter accuracy through alignment and grounding.

Open edge: Robustness under ambiguous prompts, modality shift, and complex geometry is still uncertain; evidence is limited on whether grounding choices transfer beyond curated text-to-CAD settings.
Optimization-based and stepwise reverse engineering from geometry

Recovers editable parametric CAD construction sequences from meshes or point clouds by incrementally fitting operations such as extrusions, revolutions, fillets, and chamfers, using geometric feedback or IoU-driven optimization.

Open edge: Scalability to complex assemblies, metric consistency, and comparison with end-to-end generative models beyond the reported DeepCAD, Fusion 360, MCB, and related datasets remain unresolved.
Unified CAD benchmarks and evaluation protocols

Provides multimodal, multi-task evaluation suites and rubrics that assess geometry fidelity, executability, validity, manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability across varying input modalities and output programs.

Open edge: VLM-judge reliability, dataset coverage, metric ranking instability, and accessible artifacts for controlled comparison remain verification gaps.
Agentic clarification before CAD code synthesis

Reduces ambiguous text-to-CAD failures by having a proactive agent audit prompts, ask targeted clarification questions, and produce a self-consistent specification before a coding agent generates executable CAD programs.

Open edge: Interaction overhead, generalization beyond text-to-CadQuery workflows, and separation of clarification gains from coding-agent fine-tuning effects are not fully isolated.

Shared Direction

  • CAD generation and reconstruction are predominantly formulated as structured program synthesis or parametric operation prediction rather than unstructured geometry generation.
  • The field is converging on multimodal inputs and unified evaluation, covering text, image, sketch, point cloud, and mesh conditions.
  • Executability, validity, and geometric fidelity are treated as core evaluation signals, not just sequence-level or linguistic quality.
  • LLM/code-generation approaches are central for text-to-CAD, while specialized geometric optimization still shows strong results for mesh-to-CAD under idealized inputs.
  • Grounding language to B-Rep primitives or parametric structures is viewed as necessary for improved consistency and lower invalid program rates.

Key Differences

  • Output representation differs: some methods produce CadQuery scripts, others produce construction-tree reasoning trajectories, direct B-Rep primitives, or gradually fitted parametric operations.
  • Optimization and supervision differ: some rely on supervised fine-tuning plus reinforcement learning, others use geometric feedback optimization, perturbation-based parameter grounding, or agentic fine-tuning.
  • Evaluation targets differ: geometric reconstruction metrics are primary in some work, while others emphasize engineering-ready criteria such as manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • Interaction mode differs: most systems are single-shot, while one line uses proactive clarification before code synthesis.
  • Input and deployment emphasis differs: methods specialize in text, image, sketch, point cloud, mesh, or multi-view inputs; specialized mesh-to-CAD models and general multimodal LLMs show different strengths depending on input conditions and metrics.

Open Questions

  • How can CAD-specialized performance be preserved as input modality shifts and geometric complexity, such as B-rep face count, increases?
  • What evaluation setup can fairly compare different input modalities and output representations when model rankings change across metrics?
  • How reliable are VLM-based judges for manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability beyond the limited human validation reported?
  • How much of the improvement in clarification-based systems comes from the clarification agent rather than from fine-tuning the CAD coding agent?
  • What is the replication and generalization boundary given missing code, dataset, metric, and limitation details in much of the current evidence?

Research Lines

Unified multimodal CAD benchmark construction

Open edge: Whether the curated task distribution covers real noisy, deployed, or assembly-level settings, and whether rankings remain stable across metrics and modality shifts.
Engineering-intent evaluation with design specification rubrics

Moves evaluation beyond executable geometry to manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability using staged checks and a VLM judge.

Open edge: VLM judge reliability is only partially supported by human annotation, and alignment with real downstream engineering approval remains unshown.
Geometric reconstruction and stress testing of parametric CAD programs

Recovers editable CAD construction sequences from 3D meshes and reports fidelity or validity on shared datasets.

Open edge: Generalization under increased geometric complexity and noisy or multi-modal inputs is not fully bounded by the available evidence.
Structurally grounded and interactive text-to-CAD generation

Improves structural consistency, parameter accuracy, and ambiguity handling before CAD program synthesis.

Open edge: Real-world prompt coverage, interaction overhead, and consistent benefit across different model families need further evidence.

Shared Direction

  • Parametric, editable CAD outputs are preferred over raw mesh or surface-only outputs.
  • Evaluation should combine code validity, geometric fidelity, and design-intent or engineering-quality signals.
  • Performance degrades with geometric complexity and under changes in input modality.
  • Model rankings are sensitive to the chosen metric, so single-metric conclusions are insufficient.
  • Recent work increasingly uses large language or multimodal models for CAD program generation, but specialized or stepwise methods can still outperform on idealized geometry-to-CAD tasks.

Key Differences

  • Some work evaluates mesh-to-CAD reverse engineering with geometric metrics, while other work evaluates text-to-CAD assemblies with manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability rubrics.
  • Some systems use a single unified multimodal model end to end, while others combine grounded modules, stepwise construction, or optimization over structured CAD programs.
  • Ambiguous text is handled differently: one line treats clarification as an interactive pre-generation step, while others assume a fixed prompt and focus on generation fidelity.
  • Evaluation authority varies between geometric or executable checks, design-specific rubrics judged by a VLM, and human validation reliability evidence.

Open Questions

  • Which proxy metrics align with the engineering decisions that downstream CAD users actually need?
  • Do benchmark conclusions remain stable across different implementations, compute budgets, and dataset sampling choices?
  • How reliable are VLM judges for fine-grained design intent beyond the reported human validation?
  • Does the current benchmark distribution cover noisy, real-world, and assembly-level CAD scenarios sufficiently?
  • Limitation fields and artifact availability are not reported for several entries, so boundary conditions and reproducibility remain unverified.

Unified multimodal CAD generation and benchmarking

Handles image, text, point-cloud, and sketch inputs plus multiple CAD tasks such as reconstruction, generation, and question answering in one model or benchmark.

Open edge: Robustness to real-world images and unseen operations is only partially demonstrated; artifact availability and multi-task generalization remain verification gaps.
CAD program evolution and synthetic data scaling

Mitigates data scarcity and constructs complex, realistic CAD programs by iteratively evolving or augmenting executable parametric code.

Open edge: Whether generated programs remain valid across CAD kernels and whether gains transfer beyond curated image-to-CAD benchmarks is not established.
Stepwise process supervision and sequential decision-making

Uses intermediate geometric comparisons or offline RL to reconstruct CAD action sequences and align decisions with target geometry.

Open edge: Data efficiency and transfer to noisy, real-world or manufacturing-grade inputs with broader action spaces are underevidenced.

Shared Direction

  • Parametric CAD programs are treated as the preferred editable and verifiable output representation.
  • Models are evaluated through geometric or programmability outcomes such as volumetric similarity, surface distance, invalid rate, syntax rate, or solid similarity rather than text matching alone.
  • Iterative feedback, geometric alignment, or program validation is used to improve CAD reconstruction and generation.
  • Dataset scale and synthetic augmentation are common levers for improving complex CAD generation.

Key Differences

  • Input representations and supervision differ: some methods condition on meshes or orthographic views with geometric feedback, while others use image-code or text-image pairs end to end.
  • Generation mechanisms differ: hybrid optimization, state-space sequence models, multimodal large language models, offline RL decision transformers, and program evolution each assign credit differently.
  • Evaluation targets differ: task-specific benchmarks, unified multi-task suites, image-to-CAD transfer, and independent geometric-header diagnostics emphasize different success criteria.
  • Deployment evidence differs: some papers expose open-source code, dataset, or repository signals while others leave the reproduction path incomplete.

Open Questions

  • How do current image- and mesh-to-CAD methods behave on real-world scans with noise, partial observations, or manufacturing features outside synthetic benchmark distributions?
  • Can unified multimodal CAD models maintain geometric validity and program correctness when scaling to longer, more heterogeneous CAD sequences and unseen operations?
  • What evaluation setup can avoid metric circularity while still capturing semantic design intent beyond B-rep assertion pass/fail?
  • How robust is design-intent conditioning across different model scales, CAD languages, and prefix or conditioning regimes?

Research Lines

Benchmark-driven evaluation of multimodal and programmatic CAD generation

Standardizes comparison across input modalities, output metrics, and geometric complexity for AI-assisted CAD program generation.

Open edge: Metric alignment with engineering usability, generalization to unseen part families, and robustness under input distribution shift are not yet resolved.
LLM-based executable CAD program synthesis with grounding or clarification

Converts natural language or visual input into executable CadQuery-style programs and resolves ambiguous specifications before synthesis.

Open edge: Faithfulness of fine-grained parametric operations and whether reinforcement learning transfers outside curated or fine-tuned distributions remain unclear.
Representation-centric generative modeling for parametric and B-Rep CAD

Produces editable CAD models from text, images, or point-level supervision via parametric sequences, state-space models, or differentiable Bézier patches.

Open edge: Scaling to complex assemblies, long sequences, and multimodal transfer without CAD-specific annotations is still unproven.
Engineering-oriented design-intent evaluation

Moves evaluation beyond geometric fidelity to manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability using structured specifications and judge rubrics.

Open edge: Reliability of VLM judges and the persistent failure cascade from executable code to valid geometry to engineering-ready designs remain unaddressed.

Shared Direction

  • CAD generation is treated as program synthesis plus geometry realization, so evaluation should check code executability, geometric validity, and design intent.
  • Specialized generation models or fine-tuned agents outperform general-purpose vision-language models on in-distribution CAD tasks, especially under clean inputs.
  • Benchmark stratification by complexity, modality, and part family is necessary to expose failures that aggregate metrics hide.

Key Differences

  • Representation choices differ: executable scripts, parametric sequences, latent encodings, and differentiable B-Rep patches trade off editability, fidelity, and supervision needs.
  • Supervision signals differ: some rely on point-level or reconstruction losses, while others use curated text-to-CAD pairs or execution-verified programs.
  • Evaluation scope differs: one line targets multimodal reconstruction quality, while another targets engineering requirements such as manufacturability and assemblability.
  • Interaction mode differs: most systems generate in one pass, while clarification-based agents add an interactive specification stage before code synthesis.
  • Deployment assumptions differ: idealized mesh-to-CAD settings differ from industrial part families and assembly-level design data.

Open Questions

  • How well do current CAD generation systems transfer to unseen part families and real industrial design tasks outside curated benchmarks?
  • Under what conditions do VLM-based design-intent rubrics provide reliable evidence for manufacturability and assemblability?
  • What explains ranking changes across geometric and programmatic metrics, and which metric should govern model selection?
  • Can formal geometric modeling constraints or representation assumptions predict the code-to-geometry-to-design failure cascade reported in benchmark studies?

Dataset creation for manufacturing processes

Providing a multimodal dataset (Slice-100K) for extrusion-based 3D printing, which can facilitate learning-based slicing and toolpath planning.

Open edge: The downstream model performance and the dataset's coverage of manufacturing variability are not discussed in the evidence.

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
