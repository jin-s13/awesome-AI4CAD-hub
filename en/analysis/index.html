Research Lines

Text-to-CAD program generation via LLMs with parametric/B-rep grounding

Translates natural language design specifications into executable, editable parametric CAD programs while aligning geometric primitives and structure.

Open edge: Robustness to ambiguous prompts, generalization beyond curated text-to-CadQuery datasets, and attribution of gains to representation versus base model or agent.
Geometry-to-CAD reconstruction and reverse engineering

Recovers parametric CAD construction sequences from meshes or point clouds, balancing geometric fidelity and program validity.

Open edge: Scaling to complex geometry, handling modality shift, and separating learned model effects from incremental optimization and validation.
Unified multimodal multi-task CAD modeling

Provides a single model or framework for point-to-CAD, text/image-to-CAD, and CAD question answering across input modalities.

Open edge: Whether end-to-end multi-task training maintains task-specific accuracy and how model rankings change across metrics.
CAD evaluation benchmarks and protocols

Defines multimodal, stratified, and design-intent-aware evaluation for CAD program generation and assemblies.

Open edge: Validation of engineering-readiness metrics beyond VLM judges, coverage across complex assemblies, and comparability across benchmarks.

Shared Direction

  • Parametric CAD is represented as executable programs or construction sequences, making editability and validity explicit rather than treating output as raw meshes.
  • Geometric grounding, intermediate comparison, or optimization feedback is used to improve fidelity and reduce invalid programs.
  • Evaluation is expanding beyond single geometric metrics to include executability, complexity stratification, and design intent.
  • Agentic or stepwise processes can resolve specification ambiguity or refine intermediate geometry, but reported gains often depend on final-output metrics.

Key Differences

  • Representation and supervision differ: some use CadQuery code generation with supervised fine-tuning or reinforcement learning, others use construction-tree decomposition with perturbation/ranking, and others use IoU-driven hybrid optimization.
  • Input and interaction modes differ: text-only single-pass generation, multimodal end-to-end generation, and proactive clarification before coding.
  • Evaluation targets differ: geometric fidelity and executability versus manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability versus unified multi-task performance.
  • Deployment assumptions differ: idealized mesh inputs favor specialized reconstructors, while real-world text-to-CAD with engineering intent exposes cascading failures.

Open Questions

  • How well do text-to-CAD models preserve engineering intent on complex assemblies beyond the limited success reported in current benchmarks?
  • Which model components—representation, grounding, optimization, agent, or base LLM—explain gains without full ablation evidence?
  • How robust are CAD-specialized models under modality shift and increasing B-rep face count, given benchmark-documented ranking instability?
  • Can VLM-judge engineering-readiness metrics be validated as reliable and aligned with real manufacturability and assemblability outcomes?
  • Several included papers leave limitations empty, so boundary conditions and failure modes are underreported in this evidence packet.

Research Lines

Benchmark task and dataset design

Builds shared CAD evaluation suites spanning text, image, mesh, sketch, and point-cloud inputs for program generation, reverse engineering, and QA, so different model families can be compared under one protocol.

Open edge: Visible evidence does not establish coverage of real assembly workflows, ambiguous designer intent, or noisy production inputs; several benchmarks lack visible code, dataset, licensing, and limitation details.
Metric and stress-test design

Probes geometric validity, executability, compactness, modality robustness, and engineering criteria such as manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability beyond aggregate generation success.

Open edge: Proxy metrics have not been shown to align with downstream engineering or manufacturing decisions; rubric/VLM-judge reliability and cross-benchmark comparability remain limited.
Comparable baselines and reporting

Provides multi-model, multi-metric rankings and staged evaluation protocols that reveal failure cascades from code to geometry to design intent.

Open edge: Rankings change across metrics and modality shifts; missing implementations, datasets, compute details, and limitation statements prevent verification of stability and reproducibility.

Shared Direction

  • Shared CAD benchmarks are moving toward unified multimodal suites that compare specialized parametric/B-Rep models with LLM/VLM code-generation baselines.
  • Evaluation increasingly separates syntax-level success from geometric validity and design-intent quality, using staged checks, IoU/Chamfer, invalid ratio, and rubric-based judges.
  • No single metric is treated as sufficient: model rankings shift across modalities, geometric complexity, and metrics, so multi-dimensional reporting is necessary.
  • Specialized CAD models remain strong under clean or idealized inputs, while LLM/VLM and agentic approaches are tested for robustness and engineering readiness.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation targets differ: idealized mesh-to-CAD reverse engineering, text-to-CAD assembly generation, and multi-task CAD QA impose different validity criteria.
  • Representation and supervision differ: parametric construction sequences, CadQuery scripts, B-Rep primitive grounding, stepwise feature fitting, and clarification trajectories are used as alternative abstractions.
  • Metric emphasis differs: some works privilege geometric aggregate measures, while others foreground manufacturability/function/assemblability and code-to-geometry failure cascades.
  • Interaction mode differs: zero-shot generation benchmarks versus proactive clarification before code synthesis imply different evaluation of user intent and interaction cost.

Open Questions

  • Do current geometric and rubric/VLM metrics align with CAD engineers' downstream decisions about editability, manufacturability, and assembly?
  • Are multi-metric rankings stable across implementations, compute budgets, data splits, and unseen modality shifts when artifacts are incomplete?
  • How should benchmarks measure ambiguous or under-specified text prompts without letting interaction overhead dominate?
  • What evaluation designs can cover noisy production meshes, complex assemblies, and real-world design specifications beyond curated academic splits?

Research Lines

Executable text-to-parametric-CAD generation

Converts natural-language design specifications into editable CAD construction sequences or scripts, aiming for structural consistency and parameter accuracy.

Open edge: Robustness under ambiguous prompts, exact grounding to B-Rep primitives, and generalization beyond curated text-to-CadQuery datasets or user settings.
Geometry-to-parametric-CAD reverse engineering

Recovers editable parametric CAD operation sequences from meshes, images, sketches, or point clouds while improving geometric fidelity and program validity.

Open edge: Degradation under modality shift and growing geometric complexity, and stable unification of image-to-CAD with mesh-to-CAD workflows.
Unified multimodal CAD benchmarks and evaluation systems

Provides standardized evaluation across CAD tasks and input modalities, adding code executability, geometric validity, manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability checks.

Open edge: Metric disagreement, the failure cascade from executable code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design, and independent validation of rubric-based or VLM judges.
Interactive and agentic CAD systems

Clarifies ambiguous specifications before program synthesis to reduce invalid outputs and geometric error while limiting interaction overhead.

Open edge: Closed-loop latency, fidelity-controllability-complexity trade-offs, integration with real CAD kernels, and scaling to complex assemblies.

Shared Direction

  • Executable CAD programs or construction sequences are treated as the primary output for editable and controllable generation.
  • Evaluation is moving beyond geometric distance toward code executability, geometric validity, and engineering design intent.
  • Language, image, sketch, point-cloud, and mesh pathways are being fused into unified or LLM-driven pipelines, with structure alignment and parameter grounding as recurring mechanisms.
  • Most evidence relies on curated datasets or benchmarks and uses supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, or structured optimization to improve generation quality.

Key Differences

  • Input scope differs: some systems handle text prompts only, while others reconstruct from meshes, images, sketches, or point clouds.
  • Generation mechanism differs: LLM code synthesis, transformer-based primitive grounding, IoU-driven hybrid optimization, and stepwise feature construction coexist.
  • Evaluation target differs: geometric fidelity and program validity are central in some work, while downstream manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability are central in others.
  • Interaction mode differs: one-shot generation contrasts with proactive clarification that requests missing specifications before synthesis.
  • Deployment setting differs: benchmark-suite evaluation dominates, with limited evidence for external CAD-kernel deployment or real-time serving.

Open Questions

  • How well do these systems transfer from curated benchmarks to real user prompts, external CAD kernels, and changing deployment infrastructure?
  • How should model choices be made when rankings change across geometric fidelity, executability, and program compactness?
  • What interaction budget and latency are acceptable for proactive clarification in closed-loop CAD workflows?
  • Can engineering-oriented rubrics and VLM judges be independently reproduced across teams, datasets, and evaluation pipelines?
  • How can complex B-Rep assemblies be handled beyond the observed failure cascade from executable code to valid geometry to design-intent alignment?

Parametric mesh and point-cloud reverse engineering

Recovering editable parametric CAD construction sequences from mesh or point geometry while improving geometric accuracy and program validity.

Open edge: Transfer across heterogeneous complex industrial geometry is not fully evidenced; several top results lack detailed limitation statements and broader cross-dataset validation.
Unified multimodal multi-task CAD generation

Handling point-to-CAD reconstruction, text/image-to-CAD generation, and CAD question answering within a single framework.

Open edge: Evidence is limited on generalization outside the reported benchmark suite; artifact and code availability signals are weak for the unified model.
Direct image-to-code generation with vision-language models

Producing editable CAD code directly from visual input by fine-tuning open-source VLMs on image-code pairs.

Open edge: Initial generalization to real-world images and unseen CAD operations is only partial, leaving distribution-shift robustness unresolved.
Evolutionary program and dataset generation

Growing complex parametric CAD programs from simple primitives to create large script-geometry datasets for downstream image-to-CAD training.

Open edge: Whether generated data improves downstream design validity beyond benchmark metrics or transfers to fabrication/simulation workflows is not established.

Shared Direction

  • Recent work converges on parametric CAD programs or scripts as the preferred output because they preserve editability and enable executable or geometric validation.
  • Geometric similarity and validity metrics, such as volumetric IoU, Chamfer distance, invalid ratio, syntax validity, and B-rep assertions, are used across the evidence as core evaluation signals.
  • Benchmarks and datasets such as DeepCAD, Fusion 360, MCB, and newly proposed datasets anchor evaluation, but no single consistent evaluation protocol spans all approaches.
  • The visible top evidence is shifting toward multi-modal inputs and generative modeling rather than purely geometry-driven reverse engineering.

Key Differences

  • Formulation differs: some approaches solve CAD recovery via optimization over parametric programs, others via stepwise feature construction, latent generative modeling, offline reinforcement learning, direct vision-language code generation, or evolutionary data generation.
  • Supervision and training signals differ: geometric alignment rewards, self-supervised reconstruction, image-code supervised fine-tuning, GAN-based latent generation, and causal diagnostic controls imply different assumptions about what drives valid CAD output.
  • Evaluation targets differ across reconstruction accuracy, multi-task benchmark breadth, valid code syntax, sequence reconstruction, and conditional design-intent adherence, making direct comparison difficult.
  • The role of design-intent conditioning is contested: one diagnostic study shows wrong headers can harm adherence and apparent correct-header benefits shrink under an independent metric, challenging simpler benchmark conclusions.

Open Questions

  • Do reported SOTA improvements on DeepCAD, Fusion 360, MCB, and new CAD benchmarks transfer to real manufacturing parts, CAE/CAM pipelines, or 3D printing constraints?
  • How robust are current geometric and validity metrics given the evidence that a header-adherence metric can be circular and overstate conditioning benefits?
  • Several top methods have missing limitation statements or weak code/dataset artifact signals; what verification gaps remain before these results can be independently reproduced?
  • Can unified multimodal models and generated synthetic datasets handle unseen CAD operations, real-world image noise, and longer parametric sequences beyond the tested distributions?
  • Does better CAD sequence reconstruction or code generation produce materially better editable designs, manufacturability, or simulation readiness?

Research Lines

Evaluation-driven formalization of CAD success criteria

Defines measurable checks for program executability, geometric validity, and engineering intent such as manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.

Open edge: Whether changing metrics and rubric-based VLM judgments capture engineering readiness across unseen part families and complex design specifications.
LLM-based program synthesis for CAD

Translates natural-language or multimodal prompts into executable parametric CAD programs such as CadQuery scripts.

Open edge: Faithful recovery of fine 3D structure and complex operations such as sweeps or lofts, and generalization beyond in-distribution industrial part families.
Hierarchical structure and parameter grounding

Aligns language structure with CAD construction trees and grounds parameters to reduce topology and parameter errors in text-to-CAD generation.

Open edge: Whether perturbation-based and ranking-based supervision avoids shortcut learning beyond evaluated CAD sequence generation and reconstructed model benchmarks.
Differentiable and self-supervised CAD representation learning

Generates long parametric CAD sequences or editable B-Reps from point-level or reconstruction supervision without requiring CAD-specific annotations.

Open edge: Robustness under geometric complexity and modality shift is only partially tested; validity and engineering usability of generated programs remain verification gaps.

Shared Direction

  • All high-rank evidence treats CAD as structured, editable output such as parametric command sequences or B-Rep/parametric surfaces rather than only unstructured meshes.
  • Work converges on layered evaluation: code validity, geometric fit, and engineering or design-intent feasibility, with failures increasing across these stages.
  • Across methods, performance degrades under geometric complexity, modality shift, and ambiguous or under-specified user intent.
  • Program executability and geometric fidelity are repeatedly treated as necessary but not sufficient conditions for manufacturable or functional CAD.

Key Differences

  • Representation differs: executable CadQuery scripts, hierarchical construction-tree sequences, B-Rep parametric patches, and latent state-space representations make different structural assumptions.
  • Supervision differs: text prompts, point-level supervision, CAD reconstruction, and curated text-to-program datasets impose different learning signals and data requirements.
  • Evaluation targets differ: some work emphasizes geometric distance and program executability, while others targets manufacturability, functionality, assemblability, or program compactness.
  • Interaction mode differs: direct one-shot generation versus proactive clarification before CAD code synthesis.
  • Architecture differs: LLM/transformer, Mamba state-space with GAN decoding, and differentiable parametric-surface frameworks make different assumptions about sequences and geometry.

Open Questions

  • How well do current CAD metrics and rubric-based judges align with fine-grained design intent and engineering readiness?
  • Can models generalize to unseen industrial part families and complex operations after fine-tuning or reinforcement learning?
  • Which representation or supervision choice improves robustness to geometric complexity and modality shift?
  • Are reproducibility and limitation evidence sufficient to verify cross-benchmark claims, given uneven visibility into code and artifacts?
  • What formal assumptions about CAD programs or B-Rep structure are necessary for controllable, valid generation beyond the reported benchmarks?

Research Lines

B-rep generation and geometric modeling

Generating topologically valid and geometrically complex boundary representation models, which are the standard in industrial CAD.

Open edge: Generalization to other CAD formats and verification on large-scale industrial datasets; the provided evidence does not discuss limitations or failure modes.
3D reconstruction from point clouds

Reconstructing structured 3D building models from unstructured point clouds, potentially using polyhedron-based graph neural networks.

Controllable CAD model generation

Enabling user control over the generation process through hierarchical neural coding, which could support design exploration.

Open edge: The evidence lacks details on the control mechanisms, evaluation metrics, and any constraints on the types of controllable edits.
Dataset creation for manufacturing processes

Providing a multimodal dataset (Slice-100K) for extrusion-based 3D printing, which can facilitate learning-based slicing and toolpath planning.

Open edge: The downstream model performance and the dataset's coverage of manufacturing variability are not discussed in the evidence.

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
