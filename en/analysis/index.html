Research Lines

Text-to-CAD program synthesis

Generating executable parametric CAD programs from natural language specifications while preserving editability.

Open edge: Ambiguous prompts, fine-grained engineering constraints, and generalization to complex assemblies are not fully covered.
Geometry-to-CAD reverse engineering

Recovering parameterized CAD construction sequences from meshes or point clouds through stepwise or optimization-based reconstruction.

Open edge: Scaling to high geometric complexity, avoiding invalid operations, and robustness under modality shift remain open.
Unified multimodal multi-task CAD modeling and evaluation

Supporting point, text, image, sketch, and perhaps other inputs while addressing multiple CAD tasks in one framework.

Open edge: Whether universal models remain competitive with specialized models under distribution shift, resource limits, and metric variation is unresolved.
CAD program optimization and grounding objectives

Aligning symbolic CAD structure with geometry and parameters through structure alignment, primitive grounding, or geometric feedback optimization.

Open edge: Which objective designs transfer beyond the reported datasets and task families is unclear.

Shared Direction

  • CAD is increasingly represented as executable parametric programs or construction sequences rather than only boundary geometry, enabling editability and validity checks.
  • Evaluation is moving beyond geometric similarity to include code executability, compactness, manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • LLMs and multimodal models are common generators, but specialized optimization, grounding, or stepwise verification mechanisms are needed for high-fidelity geometry and complex parts.
  • Benchmarks stratify by geometric complexity or design intent to expose failure modes hidden by aggregate scores.

Key Differences

  • Representation and supervision differ: direct code generation, stepwise feature construction, hybrid geometric optimization, hierarchical structure alignment, and interactive clarification all coexist.
  • Evaluation targets differ: some work optimizes geometric fidelity alone, while others prioritize code validity, invalid ratio, or engineering design intent such as manufacturability and assemblability.
  • Interaction mode differs: single-shot generation is contrasted with agentic clarification that asks targeted questions before synthesis, trading interaction overhead for robustness.
  • Task scope differs: specialized mesh-to-CAD or text-to-CAD methods report higher idealized-input performance, while unified multimodal frameworks pursue broader task coverage.

Open Questions

  • How should models maintain CAD construction fidelity when geometric complexity and input modality shift together?
  • What evaluation protocol can balance shape fidelity, code validity, and engineering design intent without rankings changing substantially across metrics?
  • How much of reported gains is attributable to method design versus dataset quality, baseline choice, metric selection, or post-hoc verification, given limited code and limitation evidence in this packet?
  • For clarification-driven and agentic systems, how much user interaction is required and what evidence supports real-world use?
  • Can unified multimodal CAD models remain competitive with specialized models on distribution shifts, rare CAD operations, and practical design constraints?

Research Lines

Benchmark task and dataset design

Establishes shared multimodal CAD evaluation tasks and dataset aggregations so text-to-CAD, mesh/point-to-CAD reconstruction, and CAD question answering can be compared under one suite.

Open edge: Whether task distributions and dataset sampling cover real engineering workflows, design intent, assemblies, and the settings where CAD generation claims are expected to transfer.
Metric and stress-test design

Adds multi-stage checks for code executability, geometric validity and fidelity, program compactness, and design-intent alignment, plus modality-shift, geometric-complexity, and ranking stress tests.

Open edge: Alignment between proxy metrics such as IoU, Chamfer distance, invalid ratio, or VLM-judge rubrics and the downstream manufacturability and functionality decisions users care about.
Comparable baselines and reporting

Supplies common baselines, benchmark suites, and reporting conventions so state-of-the-art claims can be audited across CAD-specific and multimodal or language-model methods.

Open edge: Stability of conclusions across implementations, compute budgets, artifact availability, and missing limitation reports; several top papers have no visible limitations and mixed reproducibility signals.

Shared Direction

  • Parametric CAD generation and reverse engineering are treated as a shared benchmark problem centered on recovering editable programs or construction sequences from one or more input modalities.
  • Recent benchmarks combine datasets with protocols and baselines, using staged evaluation from executable code through valid geometry to design-intent alignment rather than a single aggregate score.
  • Geometric fidelity, executability, compactness, invalid ratio, and engineering criteria are recurring evaluation dimensions; evidence shows model rankings and failure patterns vary with metric, modality, and geometric complexity.
  • Unified multimodal evaluation is becoming common, with benchmarks covering multiple CAD tasks under a single suite instead of task-specific leaderboards.

Key Differences

  • Input and supervision setting: text-prompted generation, mesh or point-cloud reverse engineering, image-based reconstruction, and multi-task multimodal instruction test different model capabilities.
  • Evaluation target: some benchmarks emphasize geometric reconstruction quality, while others prioritize design-intent alignment, manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability of B-Rep assemblies.
  • Metric and judging approach: some rely on IoU, Chamfer distance, executability, and invalid ratio; others introduce rubric-based VLM judges for engineering design criteria.
  • Model and interaction paradigm: approaches differ between direct LLM code generation, stepwise feature construction, hybrid optimization, and proactive clarification before synthesis.
  • Reporting and reproducibility posture: artifact visibility and limitation disclosure vary, making direct comparability of state-of-the-art claims uneven.

Open Questions

  • Does current CAD benchmark coverage adequately represent the downstream engineering settings and design-intent complexity where CAD generation claims are expected to be useful?
  • Are VLM-based rubric judges sufficiently reliable and human-aligned for manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability evaluation?
  • How stable are model rankings across different geometric metrics, input modalities, benchmark versions, and implementation budgets?
  • What evaluation protocols can systematically expose brittleness under modality shift and increasing geometric complexity?
  • To what extent do missing code, dataset, baseline, and limitation details account for observed performance differences and state-of-the-art claims?

Mesh/point-to-CAD reverse-engineering methods

Open edge: Performance degrades with geometric complexity and modality shift; invalid programs still occur; operation coverage and deployment outside benchmark datasets are not established.
Engineering-ready B-rep assembly evaluation

Evaluates generated assemblies beyond geometry by checking code executability, valid geometry, and alignment with functionality, manufacturability, and assemblability requirements.

Open edge: The judge is a VLM with human validation on limited cases; downstream manufacturing or physical simulation evidence is missing.

Shared Direction

  • CAD generation is treated as structured, executable program generation rather than unstructured shape prediction.
  • Evaluation must combine geometric fidelity, executability, and design-intent checks because code-valid outputs can still fail geometric or engineering criteria.
  • Specialized geometry-to-CAD methods outperform generic code-generating VLMs under idealized matched inputs, but no single model dominates across all metrics and modalities.
  • Parametric CAD operations and B-rep representations are shared substrates for editable, manufacturable outputs.

Key Differences

  • Output representation varies from CadQuery scripts to construction-tree trajectories, text-grounded B-rep primitives, and structured program sequences.
  • Learning or optimization differs: supervised fine-tuning with RL, hybrid IoU-driven optimization, progressive hierarchical alignment, and agent-based clarification embed different biases.
  • Evaluation targets differ: some optimize Chamfer distance and IoU, others emphasize executability and invalid ratio, while newer work targets functionality, manufacturability, and assemblability.
  • Interaction mode differs between one-shot generation and multi-turn proactive clarification, with unresolved effects on latency and user burden.
  • Some works pursue a universal multi-task model across input modalities, while others retain specialized mesh-to-CAD, text-to-CAD, or reverse-engineering pipelines.

Open Questions

  • Which evaluation signals best predict downstream engineering usability and manufacturability of generated CAD assemblies?
  • How well do these systems transfer when deployed with different CAD kernels, datasets, or user-specific constraints?
  • Can generic multimodal LLM or agent systems match specialized geometry-to-CAD methods without losing robustness under modality shift?
  • What is the acceptable interaction and latency budget for proactive clarification in real-time CAD workflows?
  • How can benchmarks close the gap between valid executable geometry and true design intent, physical function, and assembly constraints?

Shared Direction

  • CAD is represented as parametric programs or construction sequences, with editable operations as the target rather than plain shape output.
  • Geometric consistency and program validity or syntax are shared evaluation concerns across recent methods.
  • Paired data between non-CAD inputs and programs or code is central to training recent models.
  • DeepCAD, Fusion 360, and MCB benchmarks recur as common evaluation settings.

Key Differences

  • Input coverage differs: some methods reconstruct from meshes or point clouds, while others condition on images, sketches, or text.
  • Optimization and supervision differ: hybrid geometric optimization, offline reinforcement learning, VLM fine-tuning, program evolution, and state-space or GAN-based generation are all present.
  • Program representation differs between native parametric operation sequences and executable CadQuery Python code.
  • Evaluation differs between surface-similarity or validity metrics and executable geometric assertions used for diagnostic contrast.
  • Generalization claims differ: some works report state-of-the-art benchmark scores, while one evidence point shows only partial real-world and unseen-operation generalization.

Open Questions

  • Do reported mesh and image-to-CAD gains transfer to real-world scans, manufacturing constraints, CAE/CAM workflows, and unseen CAD operations?
  • What are the specific failure modes for complex or multi-feature parts when limitation fields are often missing in high-ranked evidence?
  • Can unified multi-modal CAD models retain task-specific accuracy and provide reproducible artifacts beyond reported benchmark wins?
  • Does design-intent conditioning remain harmful or beneficial across other models, longer prefixes, and diverse CAD domains?
  • Are current geometric-similarity metrics circular or biased toward surface appearance, and do executable assertions align with engineering validity?

Provides stratified multimodal samples and staged metrics to compare generators on geometry, executability, code QA/editing, and engineering intent.

Open edge: Unseen part family generalization is limited; metric rankings shift; VLM-judged design intent has limited human or physical validation; several artifacts are not clearly reproducible.
Text-to-CAD program synthesis with grounding and alignment (FutureCAD, HierCAD, ProCAD)

Converts natural language specifications into executable CadQuery or parametric construction trees, using primitive grounding, structure alignment, or clarification to reduce invalid outputs and ambiguous specification errors.

Open edge: Fine-grained parameters and complex operations are still misinferred or simplified; clarification interaction overhead and broader coverage of design specifications are not fully evidenced.
Geometry-native generative representations for CAD (DreamCAD, Mamba-CAD)

Learns latent or parametric surface representations, enabling generation from point-level or self-supervised signals and longer CAD sequences without dense CAD-specific paired annotations.

Open edge: Brittleness under modality shift and geometric complexity is observed; engineering constraints and real-world editability remain mostly unevaluated; code or dataset availability is limited.
Formal design constraints and evaluation rubrics for CAD generation

Defines code check, geometric check, and design-intent rubrics for manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability, making engineering-oriented requirements measurable beyond geometric similarity.

Open edge: Whether rubrics correspond to formal engineering validity or manufacturing outcomes is not established; benchmark coverage and judge reliability need further validation across unseen industrial diversity.

Shared Direction

  • Executable CAD programs or editable B-Reps are preferred targets over raw meshes because they support downstream engineering manipulation and constraint checking.
  • Staged evaluation is necessary because models often produce executable code but fail valid geometry or design intent, a recurring failure cascade.
  • Specialized geometric grounding, hierarchical decomposition, or clarification improves robustness over direct end-to-end generation in the reported evidence.
  • No single metric or dataset family is sufficient; rankings are sensitive to modality, part complexity, and evaluation rubric.

Key Differences

  • Representation choices differ: script-based CadQuery, hierarchical construction trees, parametric Bézier surfaces, and state-space latent sequences trade editability, training signal, and sequence capacity differently.
  • Supervision strategies differ: curated text-to-CAD pairs, point-level unannotated 3D data, self-supervised reconstruction, and reinforcement learning are used, implying different assumptions about available annotations.
  • Interaction mode differs: some systems generate directly, some ground structure and parameters internally, and some ask clarification questions before generation.
  • Evaluation targets differ: geometric fidelity and executability are measured in some benchmarks, while others emphasize code QA, code editing, manufacturability, functionality, or assemblability.
  • Deployment settings differ: idealized mesh or image inputs versus industrial part families and complex assemblies, which changes generalization expectations.

Open Questions

  • How reliably do VLM-judged engineering rubrics predict real manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability?
  • Under what input shifts and unseen part families can specialized CAD generators maintain their advantage over general LLMs?
  • Which representation and supervision combination best balances editability, geometric fidelity, and training from unannotated or weakly supervised data?
  • Can parameter grounding and proactive clarification reduce fine-grained parameter substitution errors beyond the evaluated datasets?
  • What is missing from the current evidence base: several top papers do not surface code, dataset, baseline, metrics, or limitations, so reproducibility and failure boundaries remain verification gaps.

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
