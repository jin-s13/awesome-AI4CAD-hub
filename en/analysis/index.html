Research Lines

Unified multimodal CAD benchmarks and evaluation protocols

Provides shared samples, modalities, and metrics to compare CAD program generation and expose failure modes across code executability, geometry, and design intent.

Open edge: Whether rankings and failure patterns transfer beyond the aggregated datasets; missing reproducibility details for several benchmarks are verification gaps.
LLM-based text-to-CAD program synthesis with structure and parameter grounding

Converts natural-language prompts into executable parametric CAD scripts or construction trees, improving structural consistency and parameter accuracy.

Open edge: How much benefit comes from grounding objectives, reinforcement fine-tuning, or curated text-CAD data rather than the base LLM; limitation evidence is mostly absent.
Proactive specification clarification for text-to-CAD agents

Resolves ambiguous prompts through targeted clarification before code synthesis, reducing invalid outputs and geometric error with low interaction overhead.

Open edge: Coverage of ambiguity types and how clarification overhead scales beyond the reported text-to-CadQuery setting.
Parametric geometry-to-CAD reverse engineering via stepwise or optimization-based fitting

Recovers editable CAD operation sequences from meshes or point clouds by incremental feature fitting and geometric feedback, improving validity and fidelity.

Open edge: Whether IoU and Chamfer metrics capture editability; scalability to more operations, assemblies, and noisy multi-view inputs is underevidenced.

Shared Direction

  • Current methods represent CAD as executable parametric programs or construction sequences rather than raw geometry alone.
  • Evaluation increasingly spans multiple input modalities and combines executability, geometric fidelity, and design-intent checks.
  • Recent evidence shows a failure cascade from syntactically valid code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design.
  • Specialized geometry-to-CAD approaches can outperform general code-generating LLMs under idealized inputs, while LLM-based methods are pushed toward broader language and multi-task interfaces.

Key Differences

  • Interaction mode: one-shot generation versus proactive clarification before program synthesis.
  • Supervision and optimization: supervised fine-tuning plus RL, structured alignment and ranking, agentic fine-tuning, or geometric IoU-driven optimization.
  • Evaluation target: geometric fidelity and executability versus manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability versus multi-task coverage.
  • Input and output focus: text-to-CAD with B-Rep grounding or CadQuery scripts, geometry-to-CAD reverse engineering, or unified multimodal CAD tasks.

Open Questions

  • What benchmark metrics and model rankings remain stable when datasets grow beyond current sampled and stratified test sets?
  • How can text-to-CAD models preserve both program validity and engineering intent under ambiguous prompts without excessive clarification?
  • Do current reverse-engineering gains transfer to complex assemblies, noisy or multi-view inputs, and richer operation sets?
  • Which missing code, dataset, baseline, and limitation details should be treated as verification gaps rather than evidence of absence?

Research Lines

Unified multimodal CAD benchmark and task design

Comparing CAD program generation and reconstruction across text, image, mesh, and point cloud inputs through shared tasks, stratified samples, and multiple metrics.

Open edge: Whether the task distribution covers practical engineering constraints and whether model rankings remain stable across modality shifts and metric choices.
Geometric validity and reverse-engineering evaluation

Measuring mesh/image-to-CAD reconstruction fidelity and program validity through geometric metrics such as IoU, Chamfer distance, and invalid program ratio.

Open edge: Whether these geometric and executability metrics capture editable, manufacturable output and scale to complex mechanical parts.
Design-intent, manufacturability, and assembly readiness evaluation

Assessing generated B-Rep assemblies against structured design specifications using staged code, geometry, and design-intent checks with rubric-based judging.

Open edge: Reliability and calibration of VLM judges for fine-grained engineering acceptance, and whether the protocol generalizes beyond curated design cases.
Interaction-aware robustness evaluation for text-to-CAD

Evaluating ambiguous text prompts by adding agentic clarification before CAD program synthesis and measuring validity and geometric error reduction.

Open edge: Interaction overhead, coverage of real ambiguous prompts, and whether clarified specifications generalize beyond curated text-to-CadQuery data.

Shared Direction

  • Recent evaluations treat CAD generation as a composite capability rather than a single aggregate score, using multiple metrics or staged checks.
  • They compare specialized CAD or reconstruction models with general LLM/VLM methods to reveal complementary failure modes.
  • Dataset construction is informed by stratification, diversity sampling, or practical design specifications to challenge models beyond simple objects.
  • Program executability and geometric validity remain core criteria even when higher-level design intent is evaluated.
  • Several top papers provide methodology and result evidence but do not show detailed limitations, leaving evaluation ceiling and artifact completeness as verification gaps.

Key Differences

  • Evaluation target: some benchmarks focus on geometric fidelity and program executability, while others prioritize manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • Input and task coverage: some use a single framework to cover point/image/text/CAD QA, while others isolate image/mesh-to-CAD or text-to-CAD boundaries.
  • Measurement method: geometric metrics like IoU, Chamfer distance, and invalid ratio differ from rubric-based VLM judging for design criteria.
  • Interaction setting: most benchmarks assume a single generation turn, while one evidence path introduces proactive clarification and changes the evaluation protocol.

Open Questions

  • Do current CAD benchmarks cover the manufacturing and assembly constraints that determine whether generated programs are usable in practice?
  • Are geometric proxy scores aligned with downstream engineering utility, and how should validity be weighted in benchmark totals?
  • Are model rankings stable across input modality shifts, geometric complexity strata, and different metric choices?
  • Can rubric-based VLM judges reliably supplement or replace human engineering evaluation without being overfit to the judge?
  • What is the additional cost and failure mode of interactive clarification in benchmark settings?

Research Lines

Unified multimodal CAD benchmarks and evaluation protocols

Provides standardized evaluation across text, image, mesh, sketch, and point cloud inputs; tracks executability, geometric fidelity, program compactness, and design intent.

Open edge: Metric ranking stability, robustness under modality shift, learned judge reliability, and transfer from benchmark settings to deployment remain unproven.
Text-to-CAD program generation

Converts natural language specifications into executable parametric CAD scripts while aligning object-level structure and part-level parameters.

Open edge: Structural consistency and parameter accuracy under complex or ambiguous specifications remain open, as does fine-grained engineering readiness.
Geometry-to-CAD reverse engineering

Recovers editable parametric CAD construction sequences from meshes, images, or point clouds using stepwise fitting or hybrid optimization.

Open edge: Scaling to high geometric complexity, covering richer operation sets, and avoiding invalid programs for complex designs remain unresolved.
Unified multi-task multimodal CAD modeling

Handles heterogeneous CAD tasks such as reconstruction, generation, and question answering in a single end-to-end multimodal framework.

Open edge: Cross-task and cross-modality generalization beyond the training benchmarks is not yet independently established.

Shared Direction

  • Executable parametric CAD programs are a common intermediate representation because they preserve editability.
  • Executability, validity, and geometric fidelity are shared evaluation signals across generation and reconstruction systems.
  • Recent systems expose a cascading failure pattern from executable code to valid geometry to engineering-ready design.
  • Beyond supervised fine-tuning, systems increasingly use reinforcement learning, ranking-based supervision, or geometry-driven optimization to align programs with structure and geometry.
  • Benchmark evidence distinguishes specialized CAD models from general vision-language models under idealized inputs.

Key Differences

  • Task scope differs: some systems focus on text-to-CAD, others on mesh/image/point-to-CAD reconstruction, and others pursue unified multimodal multi-task handling.
  • Representation choices differ: construction-tree sequences, direct B-Rep primitive grounding, and CadQuery script generation are used across systems.
  • Supervision and optimization differ: supervised fine-tuning, reinforcement learning, perturbed parameter ranking, and IoU-driven hybrid optimization are distinct strategies.
  • Evaluation targets differ: some work emphasizes geometric fidelity and validity, while others add manufacturability, functionality, and assemblability.
  • Interaction mode differs: one-shot generation is common, but agentic work argues for proactive clarification before code synthesis.

Open Questions

  • How robust are current systems under geometric complexity and distribution shift? Evidence from CADBench shows degradation with complexity and brittleness under modality shift.
  • How can text-to-CAD systems move from executable code to engineering-ready output? MUSE evidence shows limited success on fine-grained engineering criteria.
  • How reliable are learned evaluation judges and metric rankings for design intent, manufacturability, and assemblability?
  • What are the independent reproduction and deployment gaps given missing code, dataset, metric, baseline, and limitation details in the packet?

Research Lines

Mesh-to-parametric CAD reconstruction

Recovering editable parametric CAD operations from mesh geometry using optimization or stepwise fitting, improving volumetric overlap and reducing invalid programs.

Open edge: Cross-dataset and complex feature transfer, shape semantic editability, and computational scalability remain under-evidenced.
Unified multimodal CAD modeling and benchmarking

Processing text, images, sketches, and point clouds within one model for reconstruction, generation, and CAD question answering.

Open edge: Unclear whether unified evaluation aligns with real CAD workflows or supports long-tail operations and out-of-distribution inputs.
Vision-language CAD code generation

Generating executable, editable CAD code directly from visual inputs, with high syntax validity and 3D similarity.

Open edge: Robustness beyond training distribution is only partially shown; real-world images and unseen operations lack full evidence.
Stepwise visual and geometric feedback for CAD reconstruction

Turning CAD reconstruction into sequential decisions informed by intermediate orthographic projections and alignment rewards.

Open edge: Data-efficiency and transfer across input noise, datasets, and actual design workflows remain open.

Shared Direction

  • Parametric CAD programs are preferred over mesh-only outputs because they preserve editability and structured operations.
  • Geometric feedback and stepwise construction are common mechanisms for improving reconstruction quality and validity.
  • Evaluation combines syntactic or executable validity with geometric similarity metrics such as IoU, Chamfer Distance, or B-rep assertions.
  • Recent methods increasingly use multimodal models or large-language/vision-language models for heterogeneous CAD tasks.
  • Synthetic or augmented program-geometry datasets are used to address training data constraints.

Key Differences

  • Input representation varies across mesh, point cloud, image, sketch, and orthographic projections.
  • Supervision signal differs: direct geometric optimization, stepwise view rewards, RL-based sequence decisions, program evolution, or design-intent headers.
  • Model choices differ from optimization over structured programs, state-space plus GAN generation, and offline Decision Transformer to VLM fine-tuning.
  • Evaluation targets differ: validity ratio and geometric distance, 100% syntax validity, cross-benchmark state-of-the-art, B-rep geometric assertions, or data efficiency.
  • Scope ranges from single-task mesh/image-to-CAD reconstruction to unified multi-task systems and diagnostic causal tests of model behavior.

Open Questions

  • Do mesh/image-to-CAD gains transfer across DeepCAD, Fusion 360, MCB, CADENA-Bench, and real-world industrial parts?
  • Can unified or VLM-based CAD models preserve semantic design intent and editability rather than only geometric similarity?
  • How can benchmarks avoid circularity between header extraction, program validity, and geometric evaluation?
  • How do state-space, VLM, and program-evolution methods scale to longer sequences, complex operations, and constrained industrial design?
  • Missing code, datasets, limitations, and citation edges leave reproducibility and comparative impact as verification gaps.

Benchmark-driven multimodal and industrial CAD evaluation

Standardizes comparison across input modalities, datasets, and tasks while measuring code executability, geometric fidelity, and design-intent or engineering criteria.

Open edge: Generalization beyond curated part families, agreement across metrics and judges, and verification of code, dataset, and artifact availability.
LLM-based programmatic text-to-CAD generation

Translates natural-language specifications into executable parametric scripts with structural or parameter grounding, reducing invalid code and ambiguity errors.

Open edge: Preserving complex operations such as sweeps and lofts, handling unseen design distributions, and reducing dependence on curated fine-tuning.
Parametric CAD sequence and latent generative modeling

Scales generation of long parametric CAD sequences or editable B-Reps from weak, self-supervised, or unannotated signals.

Open edge: Engineering-level constraints, robustness under modality shift, and validation beyond geometric metrics.
Differentiable B-Rep and parametric surface representation

Directly generates editable B-Reps from point-level supervision without CAD-specific annotations by connecting mesh generation to parametric surfaces.

Open edge: Complex topology and assembly manufacturability, evaluation on industrial part families, and reproducibility details.

Shared Direction

  • Executable CAD programs are treated as the shared interface for generated outputs across benchmarks and methods.
  • Grounding language or visual inputs to parametric primitives, parameters, or topology improves structural consistency.
  • Multi-stage evaluation separates code validity, geometry validity, and design-intent or engineering readiness.
  • Frontier models recover coarse outer geometry better than fine parametric structure and complex operations.

Key Differences

  • One branch uses autoregressive or LLM code generation with textual supervision, while another uses GAN, state-space, or differentiable surface generative models.
  • Evaluation targets differ: geometric fidelity, parameter sequence accuracy, and manufacturability or assemblability are each prioritized differently.
  • Representations differ: CadQuery scripts, procedural construction trees, latent CAD sequences, and B-Rep Bézier patches coexist.
  • Interaction mode differs: single-turn generation versus proactive clarification for ambiguous prompts.
  • Supervision differs: curated text-to-CAD pairs, execution-verified programs, self-supervised reconstruction, and point-level supervision.

Open Questions

  • Can models generalize to unseen industrial part families after fine-tuning and reinforcement learning?
  • Which evaluation protocol predicts practical manufacturability and engineering usability rather than only benchmark ranking?
  • How should metric-dependent ranking instability and modality shift be resolved?
  • What evidence exists for preserving sweeps, lofts, and fine design parameters under realistic input variation?
  • How much do missing limitation and artifact details in several papers limit reproducibility and failure-boundary verification?

Open edge: The evidence lacks details on the control mechanisms, evaluation metrics, and any constraints on the types of controllable edits.
Dataset creation for manufacturing processes

Providing a multimodal dataset (Slice-100K) for extrusion-based 3D printing, which can facilitate learning-based slicing and toolpath planning.

Open edge: The downstream model performance and the dataset's coverage of manufacturing variability are not discussed in the evidence.

Shared Direction

  • Deep learning is the primary tool for processing CAD-related geometric data.
  • Synthetic data plays a crucial role in training because obtaining annotated real CAD or scan data is expensive.
  • Geometric representation choice (B-rep, mesh, point cloud) is a central design decision that influences model capabilities.

Key Differences

  • Representation type: some works operate on B-rep structures, while others use meshes or point clouds, leading to different strengths in topological vs. geometric fidelity.
  • Task granularity: the papers target different stages of the CAD pipeline—generation from scratch, reconstruction from sensors, alignment with scenes, or interaction with CAD UIs—which makes direct comparison difficult.
  • Evaluation setting: some rely on purely synthetic benchmarks, whereas others incorporate real scanned data; the lack of a common evaluation protocol limits comparability.

Open Questions

  • Can a unified representation support both generative and discriminative tasks across the CAD pipeline?
  • What are the minimal annotation requirements to adapt synthetic-trained models to real manufacturing or design scenarios?
  • How should topological validity be measured and enforced in a learning-based generation system?
